# Topic 1. The importance of excursions for serving tourists

Excursions are one of the most important parts of the tourist experience: they are not just extra entertainment, but tools of education, cultural exchange, economic development and destination branding. (This note also covers Topic 15, which repeats the same theme.)

## Key definitions

- **Excursion** – an organised, guided visit that lets tourists explore the cultural, historical, natural and social sides of a destination beyond their accommodation.
- **Tour guide** – the key facilitator of an excursion; a mediator between tourists and local culture who transfers knowledge and shapes tourist satisfaction.
- **DMO (Destination Management Organization)** – an organisation that, together with tour operators, uses excursions as marketing tools for specific tourist segments.

## Main points

**Functions of excursions (why they matter):**

1. **Experience and emotion** – excursions actively engage tourists with the local context (unlike passive resort leisure). They give a "sense of place" and turn a passive visit into a memorable journey.
2. **Education and interpretation** – professional guides give curated, credible information; this fights stereotypes and builds cultural awareness. Especially important in heritage, museum, religious and ecological tourism.
3. **First and lasting impressions** – tourists often have little time, so one excursion can define their whole image of a country or city. Good tours bring positive word-of-mouth and repeat visits; bad tours harm the destination's reputation.
4. **Economic role** – jobs for guides, drivers, translators, artisans, food vendors, performers, tour managers; support for micro-enterprises and peripheral areas; a tool for inclusive income, poverty alleviation and heritage preservation.
5. **Spatial spread and visitor-flow management** – excursions take tourists beyond city centres and resorts, diversify supply and reduce pressure on over-visited sites (overtourism).
6. **Destination branding and differentiation** – excursions show the unique character of a place and act as "instruments of storytelling".
7. **Personalisation and segmentation** – culinary tours, literary walks, ghost tours, architectural trails, eco-excursions, adventure trips.
8. **Sustainability** – raising awareness of environmental and cultural preservation (protected areas, UNESCO sites).
9. **Intercultural dialogue and peacebuilding** – meeting other ways of life challenges stereotypes; in post-conflict areas tours support reconciliation.
10. **Seasonality management** – off-season workshops, winter village tours, indoor heritage walks; inland trips from coastal resorts extend the season.

**Risks of poor management:** overcrowding, environmental damage, cultural insensitivity, dissatisfaction, and "tourist fatigue" from repetitive, standardised tours. Success depends on guiding quality, safety, group size, duration and interpretation methods. Solution: innovation, customisation, periodic evaluation.

**Requirements for guides:** communication, group management, interpretation, adapting to the audience; also foreign languages, group dynamics, inclusive communication, digital tools. Certification, licensing and quality assurance protect consumers.

**Modern trends:** after the pandemic – preference for outdoor, small-group, personalised, low-impact trips. Digital tools: online booking, mobile audio guides, GPS, VR previews, virtual tours. Technology should complement, not replace, human interaction.

## Examples

- **Kazakhstan:** Almaty as a city with a complex history that excursions help to explain; national standards for guides (history, culture, languages, safety); new specialised training programmes for guides; promoting rural and ecological excursions beyond Almaty and Astana to support regional development.
- **Emotional/educational:** walking tour in Rome; Samarkand and the Silk Road legacy; Auschwitz, Hiroshima Peace Memorial, Robben Island.
- **Economic:** village excursions in northern Thailand; indigenous Andean villages in Peru.
- **Spatial spread:** Golden Circle in Iceland; wine tours in France and Argentina; Murano and Burano islands in Venice.
- **Branding:** Kyoto heritage walk, flamenco in Seville, Amsterdam canal boat tour.
- **Sustainability:** eco-excursions in Costa Rica.
- **Case studies:** Florence (Uffizi plus art workshops), Cape Town township tours, K-drama location tours in South Korea; reconciliation tours in Bosnia-Herzegovina, Rwanda, Northern Ireland.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Start with a definition: an excursion is an organised guided visit and a core part of the tourism product, not just entertainment.
- List 5–6 functions: educational, emotional, economic, spatial (flow management), branding, sustainability, intercultural.
- Give one example per function, including at least one from Kazakhstan.
- Mention risks of poorly managed excursions and the central role of the trained, certified guide.
- Finish with modern trends: personalisation, small groups, digital tools that complement the guide.
