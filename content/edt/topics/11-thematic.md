# Topic 11. Peculiarities of Conducting Excursions on Various Topics

Each excursion theme has its own requirements: content, narrative structure, ethics, emotional tone and tools. A good guide masters general methods and also develops topic-specific literacy and adapts to the type of audience.

## Key definitions

- **Thematic specificity** — the features of an excursion that depend on its subject; it affects the style of presentation, depth of content, emotional engagement and tools.
- **Dark tourism excursions** — tours to sites of tragedy, death or suffering (former prisons, genocide memorials, disaster zones, cemeteries).
- **Audience typology** — the types of tourist groups (school, senior, academic, luxury, mass), each needing a different method.

## Main points

| Type of excursion | Main peculiarity | Guide's key task | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Historical** | Balance factual accuracy and narrative appeal; sensitive issues (colonialism, war, repression) | Ethical neutrality, source-based commentary | Samarkand: Silk Road exchange, Persian and Turkic architecture |
| **Artistic and architectural** | Visual interpretation of static objects | "Translate" visual language into words; avoid information fatigue | Hermitage: Baroque vs. Neoclassicism |
| **Scientific and natural** | Complex terminology | Didactic clarity, analogies; environmental education | Iceland: magma pressure compared to a shaken soda bottle |
| **Religious** | Historical, spiritual and artistic dimensions together | Neutrality, respect; explain dress codes and conduct | Jerusalem's Old City: multi-faith tour |
| **Industrial and technical** | Practical orientation, technical vocabulary; linear structure (introduction – observation of the process – Q&A – conclusion) | Simplify without oversimplifying; safety | Brewery in Germany: fermentation, machinery, quality control |
| **Ethnographic and cultural** | Cultural sensitivity, no stereotypes; local life is not a spectacle | Explain internal logic and values; involve community members with consent and compensation | Yurt visit in the Kazakh steppe |
| **Dark tourism** | Emotional intensity | Reflection, not entertainment; no sensationalism; testimonies, archives, verified statistics | Auschwitz-Birkenau |
| **Political and ideological** | Contested narratives, propaganda | Separate facts, interpretations and opinions; neutrality | Soviet legacy in Almaty, memory politics |
| **Literary and cinematic** | Mix of real and imagined worlds | Theatrical techniques, costumes, props; use fans' emotions | Harry Potter tour in Edinburgh |
| **Ecotourism** | Guide as educator and steward | Enforce rules, risk management, first aid | Protected wetland in Malaysia: silent observation, no littering |

**Audience profiles**

- **School groups** — pedagogical adaptation, gamified formats, age-appropriate content.
- **Senior tourists** — slower pacing, comfortable logistics, more historical depth.
- **Academic groups** — analytical frameworks, references, discussion.
- **Luxury tourists** — exclusivity, customized routes, aesthetic storytelling.
- **Mass tourists** — brevity, entertainment, photo opportunities.

## Examples

Samarkand (history); Hermitage (art); Iceland geothermal site (science); Jerusalem (religion); German brewery (industry); yurt in the Kazakh steppe (ethnography); Auschwitz-Birkenau (dark tourism); Soviet legacy in Almaty (political); Harry Potter in Edinburgh (literary); Malaysian wetland (ecotourism).

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Explain that the theme changes the style, depth, emotional tone and tools of an excursion.
- Describe 4–5 types with their peculiarity and one example each (history, religious, dark tourism, ethnographic, industrial).
- Stress ethics: neutrality in religious and political tours, no sensationalism in dark tourism, no stereotypes in ethnographic tours.
- Show how methods change for audience types (school, seniors, academic, luxury, mass).
- Conclude: success is in meaningfulness and resonance, not in the volume of information.
