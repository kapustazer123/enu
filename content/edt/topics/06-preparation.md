# Topic 6. Methodology of excursion preparation

The methodology of excursion preparation is a systematic process from the first idea to the actual tour. It is based on pedagogical design, cultural interpretation, logistical coordination and audience engagement, and it turns a simple visit into a narrative-driven experience.

## Key definitions

- **Methodology of excursion preparation** – a systematic, structured process of developing and carrying out high-quality, engaging and educational excursions, from conceptual planning to execution.
- **Theme** – the main concept of the excursion; it must be relevant, coherent and adaptable to the place and the audience.
- **Excursion script (commentary)** – the interpretive text for each stop: key facts, anecdotes, visual cues and questions for the group.
- **Excursion plan** – a document describing the structure, timing, logistics and pedagogical strategies of the tour.

## Main points

**Stages of preparation:**

1. **Theme selection** – based on cultural significance, historical importance, tourist demand and destination marketing goals.
2. **Research** – historical data, cultural facts, legends, local narratives, visuals; sources: archives, scholarly literature, museum collections, interviews with local experts. Quality of research = guide's credibility.
3. **Route design** – spatial layout, distances, accessibility, safety, time. Logical progression: introductory object, stops that develop the theme, strong conclusion. Each stop is checked for relevance, group capacity, services, seasonal conditions.
4. **Writing the script / commentary** – adapted to knowledge level, language and culture; principles: clarity, coherence, emotional appeal, interactivity; includes introductions and transitions; avoids information overload.
5. **Excursion plan** – time schedule, emergency contacts, transport, permissions, equipment list, special needs; backup scenarios (weather, traffic, closures).
6. **Pre-tour coordination** – tickets, transport, guides or interpreters, permits from municipal authorities; mandatory advance approval for factories and research centres; instructions to participants (meeting point, clothing, equipment, safety, behaviour).
7. **Training and rehearsal** – walking the route, testing timing, practising delivery; peer feedback and self-evaluation; preparing for questions, conflicts, emergencies; voice, physical and emotional readiness.
8. **Visual and material aids** – maps, diagrams, historical photos, replicas, brochures, apps, AR; checking microphones, audio guides, screens.
9. **Ethical and cultural sensitivity** – avoid stereotypes, politically sensitive comments, offensive humour; consult local representatives at minority or indigenous heritage sites.
10. **Evaluation and feedback** – surveys, group discussions, observation; use results to update content and logistics.

The conclusion of the lecture summarises the main phases as: **theme selection – route design – commentary writing – coordination – rehearsal – evaluation**.

## Examples

- **Kazakhstan / Central Asia:** an excursion on Soviet-era monuments in Almaty requires research into 20th-century politics, symbolism and local views of Soviet history; nomadic heritage sites in Central Asia – involving local hosts and artisans adds authenticity and supports community-based tourism.
- Istanbul themes: "The Architectural Legacy of the Byzantine and Ottoman Empires" or "Spiritual Life along the Bosphorus".
- Berlin route: Alexanderplatz – East Side Gallery and government quarter – Potsdamer Platz.
- Garden walking tour with an indoor backup (exhibition or café) in case of rain.
- Test run in Old Jerusalem revealing obstacles and timing problems.
- Florence: printed visuals comparing modern views with historical artworks.
- Feedback case: tourists cannot hear the guide in traffic, so audio systems are added or the route is changed.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Define the methodology and its foundations (pedagogy, interpretation, logistics, audience engagement).
- Go through the stages in order: theme, research, route, script, plan, coordination, rehearsal, aids, ethics, evaluation.
- Explain the difference between the script (what is said) and the plan (organisation document).
- Give the Almaty Soviet monuments example for research and the Berlin example for route logic.
- Stress that feedback closes the cycle and improves future excursions.
