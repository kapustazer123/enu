# Topic 5. Excursion objects and their types

Excursion objects are the core building blocks of any excursion: the route and thematic content are built around them. They can be material or intangible, and their type determines how the guide interprets them.

## Key definitions

- **Excursion objects** – material and immaterial components of the destination that have cultural, historical, natural, aesthetic, scientific or symbolic significance and are selected for interpretation and presentation to visitors.
- **Intangible (abstract, symbolic) objects** – legends, myths, local personalities or historical events that the guide brings to life through storytelling and dramaturgy.

## Main points

Objects can be classified by physical nature, cultural value, functionality, accessibility and role in the narrative. The lecture names **nine types**:

| Type | What it includes | Guide's approach |
|---|---|---|
| Historical and architectural | Ruins, fortresses, castles, churches, palaces, monuments, cemeteries, bridges, urban ensembles | Tangible link to the past, events and personalities |
| Natural | Landscapes, geological formations, rivers, mountains, flora and fauna, protected areas | Scientific knowledge, environmental ethics; often defines the whole route |
| Cultural and ethnographic | Traditional houses, craft workshops, instruments, clothing, rituals, oral traditions | Cultural sensitivity, cooperation with local communities |
| Artistic and museum | Paintings, sculptures, installations, archaeological finds, exhibits | Context, aesthetic analysis, interpretation |
| Industrial and technological | Factories, laboratories, hydroelectric stations, space launch sites | Active processes; coordination with host, safety and security |
| Religious | Temples, mosques, synagogues, monasteries, sacred natural sites | Appropriate dress, respectful behaviour, contextual knowledge |
| Memorial and commemorative | Memorials, war cemeteries, monuments to victims | Professionalism, empathy, ethical reflection |
| Contemporary urban | Modern buildings, creative hubs, street art, public spaces | Show modern identity and innovation (not always official heritage) |
| Intangible | Legends, myths, personalities, events | Storytelling, animating the past |

**Selection criteria for objects:** safety, accessibility, seasonality, audience relevance, educational value, visual impact, capacity for group engagement. A valuable object may still be unsuitable (too small for groups, no interpretive infrastructure). Overcrowding can damage sensitive sites, so lesser-known related objects can be added to spread visitor flows.

**Combining objects** in one route adds layers of meaning, contrast and diversity; the guide must balance the focus, time and smooth transitions.

## Examples

**Kazakhstan:**
- Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi (Turkistan) – spiritual symbol and historical monument.
- Charyn Canyon – natural object for geology and ecology.
- Kazakh aul in the Almaty region – yurt building, traditional cooking, kokpar (ethnographic).
- National Museum, "Golden Man" exhibits – ancient nomadic culture (museum object).
- Baikonur Cosmodrome – active technological site.
- Beket-Ata underground mosque, Mangystau – religious pilgrimage and heritage.
- ALZHIR memorial complex near Astana – women imprisoned during Stalin's repressions.
- Esentai district, Almaty – modern urban planning.

**World:** Colosseum (Rome), Grand Canyon, Louvre and the Mona Lisa, Vatican and Mecca routes, Berlin street murals, Dickens literary walk in London, ghost tour in Edinburgh, Samarkand (Registan, Shah-i-Zinda, silk paper workshops) as a combined route.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Give the definition of excursion objects (material and immaterial, with significance, selected for interpretation).
- List the nine types and give a Kazakhstan example for most of them.
- Explain that each type needs a different interpretation method from the guide.
- Name the selection criteria (safety, accessibility, seasonality, relevance, educational value, visual impact, group capacity).
- Finish with combining objects in one route (Samarkand example).
