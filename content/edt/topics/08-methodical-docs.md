# Topic 8. Technology for Preparing Methodological Developments in the Excursion Business

Methodological developments turn the craft of guiding into a professional, repeatable and quality-controlled process. This topic explains the stages and components of preparing them: from learning objectives and research to the script, route, digital tools, evaluation, safety and ethics.

## Key definitions

- **Technology for preparing methodological developments** — the systematic use of pedagogical, interpretative, organizational and technological tools to design high-quality, effective and culturally relevant excursions.
- **Methodological developments** — well-structured, research-based guidelines and educational instruments that guide the preparation, implementation and evaluation of excursions.
- **Instructional design** — the principle at the foundation of methodological development; it draws on education science, psychology and tourism studies.
- **Scripted excursion program** — a written plan of the excursion with its stages, timing, key talking points, aids and interactive parts.
- **Route optimization** — choosing the most efficient and meaningful path, balancing accessibility, visibility of sites, safety and thematic integrity.

## Main points

**Stages and components of the technology**

1. **Define learning objectives and expected outcomes.** They depend on the audience: schoolchildren, university students, international tourists or professional delegations.
2. **Structure the thematic content.** Base it on scientific research: literature review, historical documents, oral history and fieldwork data. Then transform it into an oral narrative using didactic principles:
   - contextualization;
   - analogy;
   - chronological progression;
   - reinforcement.
3. **Write a scripted excursion program.** It contains:
   - stages: introduction, main stops, transitions, conclusion;
   - estimated time for each part;
   - key talking points;
   - visual or material aids;
   - interactive components (role-playing, storytelling, comparison games, visual reconstruction);
   - expected audience questions and room for real-time adaptation.
4. **Optimize the route.** Use GIS, virtual mapping and route simulation software (e.g. Google Earth Pro, ArcGIS StoryMaps) to visualize the route, simulate timing and find logistical problems.
5. **Integrate multimedia and digital tools.** Audio-visual presentations, VR headsets, QR-code audio guides, mobile apps. The package must explain how to use them effectively and ethically.
6. **Create evaluation and feedback instruments:**
   - pre-tour questionnaires (expectations);
   - observation sheets for guide self-assessment;
   - tourist feedback forms;
   - quality assessment criteria.

**Other requirements**

- **Educational excursions** (schools, universities, cultural centres) must follow curricular standards: instructional goals, pre- and post-excursion tasks, assessment criteria. Here the excursion is a form of informal education, not just entertainment.
- **Professional standards and regulations**: certification of guides; national policy documents; recommendations of the World Federation of Tourist Guide Associations (WFTGA) on tour design, visitor engagement and ethical storytelling.
- **Risk management and contingency planning**: protocols for medical emergencies, weather changes and technical failures, especially for outdoor tours and vulnerable groups.
- **Cultural and ethical considerations**: respect for local communities, heritage protection, inclusive narratives, language without stereotypes, multiple perspectives.
- **Case-based learning and replication**: a tested, successful excursion is documented (script, route maps, materials, feedback) and used as a template for training and for new products.

## Examples

- **Samarkand**: for students the focus is architectural styles; for international visitors it is intercultural heritage and UNESCO protection.
- **Almaty's Soviet urbanism**: the guide must explain the socio-political meaning of the architecture to non-specialists.
- **Mountains of Georgia**: route planning must consider terrain, traffic access and weather.
- **"Layers of London"**: digital overlays on mobile devices show the historical changes of neighbourhoods.
- **Feminist history tour in Paris**: feedback shows which stories worked and which were too complex.
- **Kazakhstan**: the National Tourism Development Concept stresses innovation and quality control in guiding.
- **Natural parks in Canada**: checklists, emergency contacts and training modules for guides.
- **Colonial landmarks in India**: balanced narratives (colonial legacy and indigenous perspectives).
- **"Silk Road Cities of Kazakhstan"**: a successful excursion that can be adapted for Uzbekistan or Kyrgyzstan.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Start with the definition of methodological developments and why the excursion business needs them (professionalization, quality).
- Name the stages in order: objectives, research-based content, script, route, digital tools, evaluation.
- Add the supporting elements: educational standards, regulations (WFTGA, Kazakhstan's Concept), risk management, ethics, replication.
- Give one or two examples (Samarkand for different audiences; "Silk Road Cities of Kazakhstan").
- Conclude: the technology makes excursions professional, replicable and quality-controlled and supports the sustainability of heritage.
