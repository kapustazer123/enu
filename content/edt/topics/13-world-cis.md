# Topic 13. Development of Excursion Tourism in the World and CIS Countries

Excursion tourism grew from educational journeys and pilgrimages into a professional sector with its own institutions and markets. Globally it moves toward experience, education and digital tools; in the CIS it follows a special path shaped by the Soviet legacy and the changes after 1991.

## Key definitions

- **Excursion tourism** — a significant, evolving form of travel that shapes cultural exchange, heritage preservation and tourist experience; today a standalone domain, not just an extra part of travel.
- **Grand Tour** — aristocratic educational travel of the 18th–19th centuries that, together with academic travel, laid the basis of excursion tourism in developed countries.
- **Intourist** — a centralized Soviet organization that promoted excursion tourism (with the All-Union Society for Cultural Relations).

## Main points

**1. Global development**

- **18th–19th centuries**: academic travel, Grand Tours, later industrial-age sightseeing trips (Western Europe, North America, parts of East Asia).
- **20th century**: mass tourism; excursions institutionalized through tour operators, municipal tourism boards and UNESCO heritage programs.
- **Today**: demand for experiential and educational travel.
  - Cities: walking tours, bike excursions, cultural routes.
  - Rural and nature areas: eco-excursions, agrotourism, ethnographic experiences.
  - Technology (AR, GPS tours, storytelling apps) changes the format, especially for millennials and Gen Z.
- **Developing countries**: problems with infrastructure, service quality and training, but some (Vietnam, Mexico, Georgia) progressed; excursions used for community empowerment and cultural diplomacy.

**2. CIS: Soviet period**

- Highly institutionalized and ideological.
- Organizations: Intourist, All-Union Society for Cultural Relations.
- Programs were carefully planned, often scripted; objects: factories, monuments, museums, collective farms.
- Themes: Leninist ideology, labour heroism, patriotic military history.

**3. CIS: after 1991**

- Decentralization and diversification of services.
- Re-evaluation of national identity, religious heritage and pre-Soviet history.

**4. Common challenges in the CIS**

- uneven service quality;
- no standardized guide training;
- insufficient digitalization;
- weak integration into global tourism flows.

**5. Positive trends**

- Regional cooperation (UNWTO, CIS Council for Tourism): knowledge sharing, training, cross-border routes.
- Professionalization: guide certification in Russia and Kazakhstan with universities and associations (legal knowledge, intercultural communication, thematic specialization).
- New business models: free walking tours, private customized tours, online platforms (Tripster, Sputnik, Guruwalk).
- Digital transformation: multilingual audio-guide apps, QR-coded maps, virtual tours.
- Political narratives still influence content, so guide training and balanced methods matter.

## Examples

| Country | Focus of excursions |
|---|---|
| Italy (Tuscany) | Wineries, olive farms, art history |
| South Africa | Safaris, township tours on apartheid legacy |
| Russia | Saint Petersburg, Kazan, Vladivostok: imperial, Orthodox, ethnic themes |
| Uzbekistan | Samarkand, Bukhara, Khiva: Islamic architecture, Silk Road, Timurid history; "Discover Uzbekistan" app |
| Kazakhstan | City tours (Astana, Almaty), Turkestan heritage, Charyn Canyon, Altai; "sacred geography" routes; bilingual narration; QR-signs in Almaty |
| Belarus | WWII memory, Orthodoxy, Soviet industry: Brest Fortress, Khatyn Memorial, Stalin Line |
| Georgia, Armenia | Wine routes, medieval monasteries, mountain villages |
| Ukraine | Before 2022: Kyiv, Lviv, Odessa (Jewish heritage, Cossack history); now decline, shift to virtual tours |

Cross-border: the "Great Silk Road" initiative (Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Turkmenistan).

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Start with the origins: educational journeys, pilgrimages, Grand Tours, then mass tourism in the 20th century.
- Describe global trends: experience, education, technology; contrast developed and developing countries.
- Explain the Soviet model (Intourist, ideology) and the changes after 1991.
- Give 3–4 country examples, including Kazakhstan.
- Finish with CIS challenges and solutions (certification, digitalization, regional cooperation).
