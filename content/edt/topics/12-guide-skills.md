# Topic 12. Professional Skills of Tour Guides. Organization of Methodological Work of an Excursion Institution

The guide is the intermediary between the cultural-historical environment and the tourist, and the quality of an excursion depends directly on the guide's skills. Excursion institutions support these skills through systematic methodological work: scenarios, training, quality control, innovation and research.

## Key definitions

- **Tour guide** — an intermediary between the cultural-historical environment and the tourist who makes the experience informative, emotionally engaging, safe and organized.
- **Hard and soft skills** — a highly qualified guide must show a combination of both; the most fundamental are subject knowledge, linguistic competence and communication mastery.
- **Methodological work of an excursion institution** — work that ensures consistent and improving quality of guiding: excursion scenarios, training programs, quality control, research and innovation.
- **Methodological dossier** — a collection of all materials for a new tour (research, maps, visual aids) that guides use as a reference during training.
- **Methodologists** — specialists in an excursion institution who design thematic tours, working with historians, curators, designers and linguists.

## Main points

**A. Professional skills of a guide**

1. **Subject-matter knowledge** — history, geography, culture, art, plus special expertise for the topic (e.g. architecture: styles, construction, iconography).
2. **Linguistic competence** — fluent delivery in one or more foreign languages.
3. **Communication skills** — rhetorical structure, clear speech, adapting to audiences, non-verbal cues (eye contact, gestures, tone and pace).
4. **Emotional intelligence** — noticing fatigue, confusion or disinterest and changing the delivery.
5. **Organizational and time-management skills** — planning the route, assessing risks, monitoring group size, coordinating with transport and museum staff, timing sites and breaks; flexibility and problem-solving.
6. **Intercultural competence** — sensitivity to religious norms, etiquette and communication styles; inclusive language; stopping culturally insensitive behaviour in the group.
7. **Methodological skills** — goal setting, selecting key objects, forming thematic blocks, introduction and conclusion, interactive elements; principles of adult learning.
8. **Ethical responsibility** — authentic facts, multiple perspectives, no stereotypes, safety protocols, protection of tourists' personal data, transparency about commercial services.

**B. Methodological work of an excursion institution**

1. **Development of excursion scenarios** — methodologists design tours based on historical accuracy, audience needs and trends; materials go into a methodological dossier.
2. **Continuous professional development** — seminars, workshops, study tours, certifications; topics can be special (digital tools), general (public speaking) or legal (tourism legislation updates).
3. **Monitoring and evaluation** — tourist feedback, on-site observation by supervisors, performance reviews; criteria such as punctuality, clarity of speech, thematic coherence, group engagement.
4. **Innovation** — mobile apps, GPS tours, VR, gamification, multimedia; guidelines on using them without losing the human connection.
5. **Market research and audience analysis** — studying tourist profiles, motivations and satisfaction to adapt content, format and schedule.

## Examples

- Soviet-era architecture tour in Almaty: focus on socialist realism, site — House of Government, discussion or photo interpretation as interaction.
- A guide who takes hidden commissions from souvenir shops loses trust and breaks professional standards.
- New Silk Road heritage tour: archival research, cartography, visual aids in a methodological dossier.
- An agency in Nur-Sultan (now Astana) with a twice-yearly internal training week: senior guides share best practice; new staff are evaluated through role-play.
- In European cities guides are rated on TripAdvisor; companies use ratings for HR decisions and training.
- Data showing young tourists prefer short, interactive tours leads to walking tours with storytelling, quizzes and social media.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Define the role of the guide as an intermediary and say that quality depends on the guide's skills.
- List the skill groups (knowledge, language, communication, emotional intelligence, organization, intercultural, methodological, ethical) with a short example.
- Then explain the institution's methodological work: scenarios and dossier, training, monitoring, innovation, market research.
- Use the Almaty Soviet architecture tour and the hidden commission case as examples.
- Conclude: individual professionalism plus institutional support gives high-quality excursions.
