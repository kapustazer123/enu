# Topic 4. Structure and classification of excursions

Excursions are methodically planned cultural or educational events. Their **structure** describes the internal phases of an excursion, and their **classification** groups excursions by criteria such as theme, form of movement, duration, audience, purpose and method of delivery.

## Key definitions

- **Structure of an excursion** – the internal components and phases of an excursion.
- **Classification of excursions** – division of excursions into types according to criteria (purpose, duration, audience, content, method of delivery, etc.).
- **"Living history" excursion** – an excursion in which actors in period costume re-enact scenes at historical sites.
- **WFTGA** – World Federation of Tourist Guide Associations; its international standards rely on structured classification to define professional practice.

## Main points

**Structure – four phases:**

1. **Preparation** – choose route, theme and key stops; collect facts, maps, interpretive tools; plan sequence and logistics (transport, timing, group size, accessibility). Shapes the dramaturgical structure.
2. **Introduction** – group assembled; guide presents the theme and itinerary, builds rapport, gives context, motivates interest.
3. **Main body** – visits to the objects; information, questions, interaction; stops are thematically linked and often grow in significance. Needs time management, voice projection, body language, emotional expressiveness.
4. **Conclusion** – summary of main points, final remarks, emotional closure, recommendations, answers to questions, feedback.

**Classification of excursions:**

| Criterion | Types |
|---|---|
| Content / theme | Historical, cultural, natural, industrial, mixed (interdisciplinary) |
| Form / method of movement | Walking, bus (motorised), river and sea, air-based (helicopter), virtual |
| Duration | Short (up to 2 hours), half-day, full-day, multi-day |
| Target audience | Schoolchildren, university students, senior citizens, corporate groups, families, international tourists |
| Purpose | Educational, recreational, aesthetic, religious / pilgrimage, commemorative (many are hybrid) |
| Method of delivery | Professional human guide, audio guide, augmented reality, participatory or theatrical |

**Notes on the types:**
- Walking tours: close contact with the city, flexible pace. Bus tours: long distances, comfort for elderly people and large groups. Virtual tours: became popular during COVID-19.
- The longer the excursion, the more complex the logistics (breaks, meals, weather, energy; multi-day tours need hotels).
- New thematic types: dark tourism, LGBT history tours, street art tours, sustainable nature walks, activism-themed tours – they require ethical, responsible storytelling.
- Classification is used in university curricula, in catalogues of tour operators and DMOs, and in international standards (WFTGA).

## Examples

- **Multi-day:** a three-day excursion in the Altai Mountains (guided hikes, meetings with local communities, environmental education) – example of a multi-day tour.
- Introduction: Gaudí excursion in Barcelona starting with an overview of Catalan Modernism.
- Main body: Rome walking tour from the Roman Forum to the Colosseum.
- Conclusion: Holocaust remembrance excursion in Berlin ending with a moment of silence.
- Mixed theme: Bordeaux wine tour.
- Musical excursions in Vienna and Prague (Mozart, Dvořák).
- Dark tourism: Chernobyl, Auschwitz; favelas in Rio de Janeiro – avoid exploitation.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Define "structure" and "classification" and explain the difference.
- Describe the four phases: preparation, introduction, main body, conclusion – with one example each.
- Present the six classification criteria with 2–3 types for each (the table).
- Mention new thematic types and the need for ethics.
- Conclude with the practical use of classification (training, marketing, WFTGA standards).
