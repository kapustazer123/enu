# Topic 14. Development of Excursion Activities in Kazakhstan

Kazakhstan sees tourism as a strategic sector, and excursions help to promote cultural heritage, patriotism and the country's image. Thanks to its geography, ethnic richness and history, the country offers urban, natural, historical, religious, ethnographic and industrial excursions, but the sector still faces problems with guides, infrastructure, marketing and law.

## Key definitions

- **Excursion activity** — a form of short-term travel aimed at education, cultural enrichment and leisure; an important segment of domestic tourism.
- **"Sacred Geography of Kazakhstan"** — a national project listing sacred sites that are used for religious and pilgrimage excursions.

## Main points

**1. Historical stages**

| Period | Features |
|---|---|
| **Soviet period** | Excursions as tools of ideological education and cultural unification; organized by trade unions, educational institutions, state enterprises; focus on industry, revolutionary history, socialist values; no market flexibility, but popularized domestic travel |
| **1990s – early 2000s** | State monopolies dismantled; private initiative appears; slow growth due to economic instability, weak infrastructure, no legal and methodological framework for guiding |
| **Stabilization and revival** | Interest in cultural identity, heritage and tourism; **"Concept for the Development of the Tourism Industry of the Republic of Kazakhstan until 2023"** made cultural and historical tourism a priority (tourist clusters, support for domestic operators, training guides) |

**2. Current directions**

- **Urban** (most popular):
  - Astana — modern architecture and urban design (Baiterek, Palace of Peace and Reconciliation);
  - Almaty — Soviet heritage and ecology (Kok-Tobe, Medeu);
  - Turkestan — religious and archaeological tours (Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi);
  - Shymkent — old quarters and bazaars.
- **Natural**: Charyn Canyon, Big Almaty Lake, Borovoe, Kolsai Lakes, Altai Mountains; eco-excursions on ecology, geology and local life.
- **Ethnographic**: yurt camps, culinary workshops, falconry, storytelling on customs; in Almaty Region — horse riding, carpet weaving, national celebrations.
- **Religious and pilgrimage**: mausoleums in Turkestan, Beket-Ata underground mosque (Mangystau), Aisha-Bibi mausoleum (Zhambyl Region); Islamic architecture, legends, Sufi traditions.
- **Industrial and educational**: energy, space, mining, metallurgy; Baikonur; Karaganda and Pavlodar — former coal mines, steel factories, labour camps (memory of Soviet industrialization and repression).

**3. Challenges**

1. **Shortage of trained, certified guides**, especially in remote regions; informal tours, limited languages; no standard curricula, licensing or regulatory associations.
2. **Underdeveloped infrastructure**: transport, signage, rest areas, visitor centres, interpretation media; poor roads and sanitation at some natural sites.
3. **Weak marketing and digital presence**: few products on international booking platforms or in English; apps and virtual tours at an early stage ("Visit Kazakhstan" and regional DMO websites show progress).
4. **Incomplete legal framework**: no specific rules on guiding standards, licensing, insurance and ethics — affects safety, competition and consumer rights.

## Examples

Best practices named in the lecture:

- **"Almaty City Tour"** — guided bus tours with multilingual audio commentary.
- **"Uly Dala Eli"** in Turkestan — historical re-enactments and theatre in excursions.
- **QazaqGeography, Nomad Explorer** — private operators with customized tours for niche markets.
- Youth tourism, student volunteer guides, cooperation with schools and universities.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Start with why excursions matter for Kazakhstan (economy, heritage, patriotism, image).
- Describe the stages: Soviet period, 1990s, revival and the Tourism Concept until 2023.
- Name the directions with 1–2 sites each (urban, natural, ethnographic, religious, industrial).
- List the four challenges: guides, infrastructure, marketing, law.
- Conclude with best practices and the need for cooperation of government, business, universities and communities.
