# Topic 7. Quality of excursion service

The quality of excursion service determines tourist satisfaction, destination reputation and the long-term sustainability of sightseeing tourism. It is multidimensional: it covers the guide, the content, logistics, technology, emotions, feedback and ethics.

## Key definitions

- **Quality of excursion service** – the comprehensive excellence of planning, content, execution and customer engagement at all stages of the excursion process.
- **Emotional intelligence of the guide** – the ability to read the group's mood, respond with empathy and manage group dynamics.
- **Quality assurance** – systematic evaluation and feedback collection (surveys, reviews, interviews) followed by training or operational changes.

## Main points

**Dimensions of quality** (to be monitored constantly): guide's professionalism and competence; accuracy and relevance of content; logistical organisation; safety and comfort; interactivity and emotional resonance; match between what is promised and what is delivered.

**Components of quality:**

1. **Guide's qualification and communication** – deep knowledge plus clear, engaging, accurate delivery; adapting language, tone and content to children, seniors, foreigners or experts.
2. **Emotional intelligence and cultural sensitivity** – tourists who feel seen and respected remember the tour; avoid ethnocentric judgements; inclusive narratives; explain religious customs without bias.
3. **Content accuracy** – rich, well-researched information; no legends presented as facts; guides update knowledge with new research.
4. **Structure of content** – clear beginning, development and conclusion; storytelling, comparisons, visual metaphors, rhetorical questions.
5. **Logistics** – punctuality, transport, scheduling, crowd control, tickets, restroom and meal breaks, contingency planning; small details matter (water, shade, first aid).
6. **Technology** – apps with interactive maps, AR reconstructions, multilingual audio guides; must complement, not replace, the live guide.
7. **Emotional and aesthetic experience** – curiosity, amazement, new perspectives; passion, dramatic storytelling, anecdotes, sensory elements (music, smell, touch).
8. **Evaluation and feedback** – tour operators and DMOs collect feedback; negative feedback is handled constructively; recurring problems lead to training or redesign.
9. **Ethical and environmental standards** – avoid over-tourism, respect communities, minimal-impact behaviour; transparent pricing, fair wages for guides, support for local businesses.
10. **Training and certification** – programmes evaluate knowledge, language skills, communication style and legal awareness.

## Examples

- **Kazakhstan:** professional licensing of guides through the national tourism development programme aims to reach international standards; at the Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi guides must respect all participants' beliefs.
- Hermitage Museum, St. Petersburg – same exhibit explained differently to schoolchildren and art historians.
- Hagia Sophia, Istanbul – religious customs explained without stereotypes.
- Ancient Athens – reflect current archaeological findings, not myths.
- Prague "Architecture and Identity" walk – styles linked to political ideologies.
- Tuscany day trip – enough time per stop, traffic, comfort.
- Pompeii – digital overlays plus live narration.
- Bordeaux – wine tasting, local music, rural scenery (multi-sensory).
- Germany and Japan – strict training and cultural orientation for guides.
- **Positive case:** "Kyoto Cultural Walk" (Japan) – detail, punctuality, coordination with temples and tea houses, bilingual certified guides.
- **Negative case:** over-commercialised tours in Venice – rushing, shallow commentary, harm to fragile areas.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Define quality of excursion service and say it is multidimensional.
- Group the components: the guide (knowledge, communication, emotional intelligence), content (accuracy, structure), organisation (logistics, technology), experience (emotion, aesthetics).
- Explain how quality is controlled: feedback, training, certification (Kazakhstan licensing example).
- Add ethics and sustainability as modern quality criteria.
- Contrast the Kyoto Cultural Walk (good) with over-commercialised Venice tours (bad).
