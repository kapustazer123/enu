# Topic 10. Techniques and Methods of Conducting Excursions

Conducting an excursion is a complex professional activity. The guide must master verbal, non-verbal, interactive and organizational techniques to deliver information, keep attention and create emotional and intellectual responses.

## Key definitions

- **Narrative construction (interpretive guiding)** — turning facts into a coherent, engaging, emotional story with exposition, conflict, climax and resolution. It is rooted in the principles of heritage interpretation developed by **Freeman Tilden**.
- **Thematic segmentation** — dividing the excursion into clear thematic blocks, each with its own focus, to manage the audience's cognitive load.
- **Position-triangle** — the guide, the object and the group form a triangle, so everyone can see and hear.
- **Value-based guiding** — a method that invites reflection on cultural, ethical or philosophical themes; the guide does not impose opinions but opens space for dialogue.
- **"Tail-end" assistant** — a helper at the back of a large group who makes sure nobody is left behind.

## Main points

The lecture says a guide needs four kinds of strategies: verbal, non-verbal, interactive and organizational. (The "Type" column below is a study aid; the lecture does not label each technique.)

| Technique / method | Type | Essence | Lecture example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrative construction | Verbal | Facts become a story; humanizes history | Story of the architect of a medieval cathedral instead of a list of features |
| Thematic segmentation | Verbal / structural | Content in thematic blocks | Archaeological site: architecture, daily life, burial practices, religious rituals |
| Interactive techniques | Interactive | Q&A, quizzes, role-play, dramatization, simulation games | Battle site: group split in two for a strategic decision game |
| Visual aids and objects | Non-verbal (visual) | Photos, maps, artifacts, tablets, replicas, costumes | Kazakh nomadic heritage: felt items, ornaments, musical instruments |
| Demonstration | Non-verbal (visual) | Guide or local expert shows a process with commentary; engages many senses | Georgia: traditional qvevri wine-making |
| Site positioning | Organizational | Visibility, audibility, safety; position-triangle, facing the group, elevated spots, good acoustics | Moving groups through narrow streets or uneven terrain |
| Time management | Organizational | Timed segments, checkpoints, buffers, pacing | 3-hour city tour = 5 segments with short buffers |
| Voice and emotional modulation | Verbal / non-verbal | Tone, rhythm, volume; emotional intelligence | Holocaust tours: slow, soft; Harry Potter walks: energetic, playful |
| Technological tools | Technical | Radio guides, tablets, AR, GPS apps, digital storytelling; needs training, testing, fallback plans | Rome: AR glasses show the Colosseum in ancient times |
| Group management | Organizational | Keep group together, handle disruption, special needs, conflicts; clear instructions, body signals, countdowns | "Tail-end" assistant in large groups |
| Value-based guiding | Interactive / reflective | Open questions, multiple viewpoints, personal reactions | Memorials of political repression |
| Flexibility and adaptability | Organizational | Change route, order or length of segments without losing quality | Museum closed: go to an alternative site and include it in the story |

**Important notes**

- Interactive methods work best with young audiences, educational groups and experiential tourism.
- Visual aids need correct timing, positioning and relevance; they must support, not distract.
- With technology, the guide is both a narrator and a facilitator of the technology.
- Value-based guiding requires maturity, sensitivity and training in ethics and cultural studies.

## Examples

Medieval cathedral story; archaeological site blocks; battle site simulation; Kazakh nomadic heritage objects; qvevri wine in Georgia; Holocaust vs. Harry Potter tours; AR glasses at the Colosseum, Rome; repression memorials; a closed museum replaced by another site.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Say that techniques combine verbal, non-verbal, interactive and organizational strategies, and balance art and science.
- Describe 4–5 key techniques in detail: narrative construction (Tilden), thematic segmentation, interactive methods, demonstration, position-triangle.
- Mention organizational skills: time management, group management, flexibility.
- Give one example per technique (qvevri, Colosseum AR, Kazakh felt items).
- Conclude: guides are interpreters of meaning and facilitators of cultural dialogue, not only carriers of information.
