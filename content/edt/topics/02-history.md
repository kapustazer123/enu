# Topic 2. History of the development of excursion studies

Excursions have existed in different forms since ancient times, but excursion studies as an academic and professional field developed step by step, together with changes in mobility, education, cultural values and the tourism industry.

## Key definitions

- **Excursions** – organised and purposeful visits to specific sites of interest.
- **Grand Tour** – Renaissance-era educational travel of wealthy European aristocrats, mainly to Italy and France, to study classical art, architecture and humanist thought; a precursor of modern cultural excursions.
- **Lehrwanderungen** ("educational walks") – 19th-century practice in Germany and Russia: guided visits of students and citizens to forests, factories, museums and historical sites.
- **Excursion studies** – today an interdisciplinary field combining theory and practice (cultural representation, memory, pedagogy, sustainability, visitor behaviour).

## Main points

**Stages of development:**

| Period | Main form | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Ancient world (Egypt, Greece, Rome) | Pilgrimages, educational and leisure trips of the upper classes | Visits to temples, monuments, nature; interpretation by priests, philosophers, local hosts |
| Middle Ages | Religious pilgrimage | Collective movement, waystations, storytelling (Santiago de Compostela, Canterbury, Mecca) |
| Renaissance | Grand Tour | Galleries, ruins, universities; guided by tutors and scholars |
| Industrial Revolution (18th–19th c.) | Organised mass excursions | Railways, steamships; middle-class travel; Thomas Cook's package tours (1840s) |
| 19th century | Educational walks (Lehrwanderungen) | Preparation, thematic focus, post-excursion reflection – methodological base |
| Late 19th – early 20th c. | Institutionalisation | Society for School Excursions (1899), Excursion Bureau in Moscow (1902); methodological guides, trained leaders |
| Soviet period | Highly formalised, state-centred system | Socialist education; All-Union Bureau of Excursions and Tourism; classification, guiding techniques, textbooks |
| Western Europe / North America (mid-20th c.) | Market-oriented, interdisciplinary | Part of tourism studies; MacCannell and Cohen on authenticity and meaning |
| Late 20th – 21st c. | Globalisation, digitalisation, sustainability | New tour types, new research methods, professional training, technology |

**Thomas Cook's model:** transport, meals and guided interpretation in one package – a template for future excursion services; proved demand for structured, educational and affordable travel.

**Soviet excursion studies:** systematic classification of excursion types, thematic frameworks, guiding techniques, evaluation methods; excursion studies became both an applied discipline and a form of mass education.

**Modern period:**
- New forms: thematic walking tours, food and wine tours, volunteer excursions, dark tourism.
- New methods: qualitative research, participant observation, visitor feedback analysis, digital content creation.
- Professionalisation: special courses for guides, excursion planners and interpretation specialists.
- Technology: mobile apps, GPS audio guides, augmented reality, interactive maps, virtual tours.
- COVID-19 showed the need to rethink safety, inclusivity and hybrid formats.

## Examples

- Ancient Rome: visits to coastal villas, historical sites and public spectacles as early sightseeing.
- Thomas Cook's day trips in Britain to exhibitions, cities and seaside resorts.
- Russia: Society for School Excursions (1899) and the Moscow Excursion Bureau (1902) – excursions for patriotic and civic education.
- Scholars Dean MacCannell and Erik Cohen – sightseeing as a cultural practice, not just a commercial service.

## How to answer in the oral exam

- Define excursions and say that the practice is ancient but the academic study is relatively recent.
- Go through the stages in order: ancient world, Middle Ages, Renaissance (Grand Tour), Industrial Revolution (Thomas Cook).
- Explain institutionalisation in Russia and the formalised Soviet system (classification, methodology, textbooks).
- Contrast this with the market-oriented Western path (MacCannell, Cohen).
- Finish with modern trends: new excursion types, professional training, digital technology, post-COVID hybrid formats.
