# Topic 15. Recreational tourism project: preparation and defence

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

This is a practical guide to the final control of the course: the project defence on 26 December 2026. **The teacher's project requirements were not available when this guide was written. Check the teacher's real requirements before you start: topic (free choice or from a list), length, format (report, slides, both), citation style and deadline.**

## Key definitions
- **Recreational tourism project** — a written and presented piece of applied work. It analyses a destination and proposes a realistic recreational product, route, zone or development plan for it.
- **Defence** — a short public presentation of the project, followed by questions from the teacher or commission.
- **Recreational potential** — how suitable a territory's natural, cultural and infrastructure resources are for recreation.

## Main points

### 1. What a typical project looks like
Most projects are one of three types:
1. **Product/route design:** a tour, route or programme for one destination (for example, a 3-day eco-route).
2. **Potential assessment:** an evaluation of a destination's recreational resources, with recommendations.
3. **Zone/development plan:** a proposal for organising a recreation zone (activities, infrastructure, limits).

Choose a real destination you can find reliable information about, and keep the scope small.

### 2. Recommended structure of the written project
| Section | What to include |
|---|---|
| Title page | Topic, your name, group, teacher, year |
| Relevance | Why this destination and problem matter now |
| Goal and objectives | One goal; 3–5 objectives ("to analyse...", "to design...", "to assess...") |
| Destination analysis | Location, access, climate, seasons, current visitors, competitors |
| Resource assessment | Natural, cultural-historical and infrastructure resources; strengths and weaknesses (SWOT) |
| Target market | 1–2 segments: who, why they travel, how they book |
| Product/program design | Route, day-by-day program, activities, duration, season |
| Infrastructure and logistics | Transport, accommodation, food, safety, guides, partners |
| Carrying capacity and sustainability | Visitor limits, impact on nature and locals, mitigation measures |
| Budget and pricing basics | Main cost items, price logic (cost plus margin), break-even idea |
| Risks | Weather, seasonality, demand, safety, environment; response to each |
| Expected results | What changes for tourists, local people, nature, economy |
| References | Official websites, textbooks, articles; cite every number |

### 3. Slide-by-slide outline (7–10 minutes, 10–12 slides)
1. Title (topic, name, group) — 15 s
2. Relevance and problem — 45 s
3. Goal and objectives — 30 s
4. Destination on a map, key facts — 1 min
5. Resource assessment / SWOT — 1 min
6. Target segment (a short "persona") — 45 s
7. Product: route map and program — 1.5 min
8. Infrastructure and logistics — 45 s
9. Carrying capacity and sustainability — 1 min
10. Budget and price — 45 s
11. Risks and solutions — 45 s
12. Expected results and conclusion — 30 s

Use one idea per slide, large photos and maps, and a maximum of about 6 short lines of text.

### 4. Speaking tips
- Rehearse aloud with a timer at least three times.
- Start with the problem, not with "My project is about...".
- Speak to the commission, not to the screen; do not read the slides.
- Say numbers with their source ("according to the national park's website...").
- Finish with a clear sentence: "So, the project offers ... for ... and gives ...".
- When you do not know an answer, say so honestly and explain how you would find it.

### 5. Common mistakes
- A topic that is too wide ("Tourism in Kazakhstan").
- Only a description of the destination, with no own proposal.
- No target segment, or "for everyone".
- Numbers without sources, or invented numbers.
- No word about carrying capacity, nature protection or seasonality.
- Too much text on slides, or going over the time limit.
- Copying text from websites without citation.

### 6. Checklist before the defence
- [ ] Teacher's requirements checked (topic, length, format, deadline)
- [ ] Goal and objectives match the conclusions
- [ ] Every fact and number has a source
- [ ] Map, route and program are on the slides
- [ ] Carrying capacity, sustainability and risks are covered
- [ ] Simple budget and price are shown
- [ ] Presentation fits 7–10 minutes
- [ ] Answers to the typical defence questions are prepared (see the Questions section)

## Examples: 12 sample project topics
1. A weekend eco-tour from Astana to Burabay National Park for young families.
2. Assessment of the recreational potential of Bayanaul National Park.
3. Reducing seasonality on the Alakol lake coast: an off-season wellness product.
4. A 3-day hiking route to the Kolsai Lakes and Lake Kaindy with a carrying-capacity plan.
5. A geotourism day trip to Charyn Canyon for international visitors.
6. Recreational zoning of trails in Ile-Alatau National Park near Almaty.
7. A health tourism program at the Saryagash resort for older visitors.
8. A cultural and pilgrimage route in Turkistan region (Turkistan city and nearby historical sites).
9. An educational birdwatching product near the Korgalzhyn Nature Reserve that respects the reserve's protection rules.
10. A winter active-recreation product in the mountains near Almaty.
11. Ethno-tourism in a village in Akmola region: a guest-house stay and a program on traditional nomadic culture.
12. Short-term leisure for city residents: recreational use of Astana's parks and the Esil river embankment.

## How to answer in the exam
- Open with your topic, goal and the main proposal in 2–3 sentences.
- Show evidence: resources, segment, route, capacity, budget.
- Connect every proposal to sustainability and to the local community.
- Answer questions briefly, using numbers from your own project.
- End with the expected results and one clear concluding sentence.
