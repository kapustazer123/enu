# Topic 13. Main recreational regions and resorts of the world

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

The world's main recreational regions are formed by warm seas, mountains, unique nature and healing resources close to large markets. Europe and the Mediterranean are the historic centre of recreation; the Asia-Pacific region and the Caribbean have grown fast; mountain and spa regions specialise in winter sports and health.

## Key definitions
- **Recreational region (world level)** — a large territory with similar resources and specialisation that attracts international holidaymakers (e.g. the Mediterranean, the Alps, the Caribbean).
- **"3S" tourism** — sea, sun and sand; the classic model of beach recreation.
- **Tourism Area Life Cycle** — R. Butler's model (1980): a resort passes through exploration, involvement, development, consolidation and stagnation, followed by decline or rejuvenation.

## Main points
**1. Main types of world recreational regions**

| Type | Regions and resorts |
|---|---|
| Sea and beach (3S) | Mediterranean (Spain — Costa del Sol, Balearic Islands; France — Côte d'Azur; Italy; Greece; Turkey — Antalya; Egypt — Red Sea), Caribbean, Thailand, Bali, Maldives, UAE |
| Mountain and ski | Alps (France — Chamonix, Courchevel; Switzerland — Zermatt, St. Moritz; Austria), Rocky Mountains (USA, Canada), Scandinavia |
| Spa and health | Central Europe — Karlovy Vary (Czech Republic), Baden-Baden (Germany), Hungary's thermal baths; Dead Sea (Israel, Jordan); Caucasian Mineral Waters (Russia) |
| Nature and ecotourism | National parks of the USA and Canada, East Africa safari (Kenya, Tanzania), Costa Rica, New Zealand |
| Lake and river | Issyk-Kul (Kyrgyzstan), Lake Baikal (Russia), lakes of northern Italy |

**2. Factors that form world recreational regions**
- Natural resources (warm climate, sea, snow, healing waters).
- Closeness to large tourist-generating markets (Europe, China, Russia, Gulf countries).
- Transport (charter flights, low-cost airlines).
- Policy and image: visa regimes, safety, marketing.
- Price level and service quality.

**3. The Mediterranean** — the largest recreational region in the world: warm sea, long summer, rich cultural heritage, short distances from northern and central Europe. Problems: overcrowded coasts, water shortage, seasonality.

**4. Trends**
- Shift from pure beach rest to combined products (beach + culture + wellness).
- Growth of Asian destinations and long-haul travel.
- Climate change: less snow in low ski resorts, heat waves on beaches.
- Overtourism measures in popular cities and islands.

**5. Lessons for Kazakhstan**
- Develop all-season products (Alpine resorts work in summer as well as winter).
- Combine nature with health and culture (as Central European spa towns do).
- Protect nature with zoning and visitor limits (as US and Canadian national parks do).

## Examples
- **The Great Spas of Europe** — a UNESCO World Heritage site (2021) that includes 11 spa towns, among them Karlovy Vary, Baden-Baden, Vichy and Spa.
- **Issyk-Kul** in Kyrgyzstan is the nearest large lake resort region for Kazakhstan tourists (Cholpon-Ata).
- **Antalya (Turkey)** and **Sharm el-Sheikh (Egypt)** are popular outbound beach destinations for Kazakhstan residents.
