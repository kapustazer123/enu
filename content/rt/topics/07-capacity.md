# Topic 7. Recreational load, carrying capacity and environmental impacts

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Every recreational area can host only a limited number of visitors without damage to nature and to the quality of rest. Recreational load measures pressure on a territory; carrying capacity sets the safe limit. Too many visitors cause degradation of landscapes and conflicts with local people.

## Key definitions
- **Recreational load** — the pressure of holidaymakers on a territory, usually measured as the number of people (or person-days) per unit of area per unit of time (e.g. people per hectare per day).
- **Carrying capacity (recreational capacity)** — the maximum number of visitors an area can receive at the same time without unacceptable damage to the environment and to visitor experience.
- **Recreational digression** — the gradual degradation of natural complexes (especially forests) under recreational load.
- **Overtourism** — a situation when the number of tourists harms the destination, its residents or the quality of the visit.
- **Limits of Acceptable Change (LAC)** — a planning approach that sets the level of change that is acceptable and monitors indicators instead of fixing one number of visitors.

## Main points
**1. Types of carrying capacity**

| Type | Limit set by |
|---|---|
| Physical | Space available (e.g. square metres of beach per person) |
| Ecological | Ability of ecosystems to resist and recover |
| Psychological (perceptual) | Visitor satisfaction — crowding reduces quality of rest |
| Social | Tolerance of local residents |
| Economic / infrastructural | Capacity of water supply, roads, waste systems, accommodation |

**2. Stages of recreational digression (forest example)** — commonly five stages:
1. Natural state, almost no change.
2. Paths appear, small damage to ground cover.
3. Network of paths, trampled soil, weaker undergrowth.
4. Large trampled areas, damage to trees, no young trees.
5. Bare compacted soil, dying trees — the complex is destroyed.

**3. Environmental impacts of recreation**
- Soil compaction and erosion, damage to vegetation, fires.
- Water pollution, litter, waste water from resorts.
- Disturbance of animals and birds; noise.
- Visual pollution from chaotic construction on shores.
- Social impacts: traffic, rising prices, conflict with residents.

**4. Ways to manage the load**
- Zoning (strict areas, recreation zones), marked trails and boardwalks.
- Limiting visitor numbers, permits, online booking, entrance fees.
- Spreading visitors over time and space (off-season offers).
- Visitor education ("leave no trace"), monitoring and periodic closure for recovery.

## Examples
- Popular sites near Almaty such as Big Almaty Lake and Kolsai lakes suffer from litter and traffic in peak season; national parks manage visits with fees and rules.
- Lake shores of Burabay and Alakol face pressure from dense construction of recreation bases.
- **World:** Venice and Barcelona are classic overtourism cases; many national parks use timed-entry systems.

- List the types of carrying capacity in a table.
- Describe the stages of recreational digression.
- Name the main environmental and social impacts.
