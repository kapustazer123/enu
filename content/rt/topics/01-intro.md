# Topic 1. Recreation, leisure and recreational tourism: basic concepts

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Recreation is the restoration of a person's physical, mental and emotional strength in free time. Recreational tourism is the part of tourism whose main purpose is rest, health and renewal, and it is studied by recreational geography together with tourism science.

## Key definitions
- **Leisure (free time)** — the part of non-working time that remains after work, sleep, housework and other obligations; the person decides how to use it.
- **Recreation** (from Latin *recreatio* — "restoration") — activity in free time that restores and develops a person's physical and spiritual strength.
- **Recreational activity** — any activity done in free time for rest, health, learning or entertainment (walking, swimming, skiing, sightseeing, spa treatment).
- **Tourism** — the travel of people outside their usual environment for less than one year for leisure, business or other purposes (UNWTO approach).
- **Recreational tourism** — tourism whose main motive is rest, recovery of health and strength, usually in natural or resort settings.
- **Recreational system** — a system of interacting parts: holidaymakers, natural and cultural resources, infrastructure, staff and management.
- **Recreational geography** — the branch of geography that studies the territorial organisation of recreation and recreational resources.

## Main points
1. **Recreation vs tourism.** Recreation is wider: it includes rest at home or in a city park. Tourism always involves travel outside the usual environment. Recreational tourism is where the two overlap.
2. **Short-term vs long-term recreation.**
   - Short-term (daily, weekend) — near the place of living, without overnight stay or with one night.
   - Long-term (holiday) — several days or weeks, usually with travel and overnight stays; this is the main field of recreational tourism.
3. **Cycles of recreation:** daily (after work), weekly (weekend), annual (vacation) and lifetime (e.g. retirement travel).
4. **The recreational system (territorial recreational system).** The Soviet school of recreational geography (V.S. Preobrazhensky and colleagues) described recreation as a system of five sub-systems: holidaymakers, natural and cultural complexes, technical systems (infrastructure), service staff and the management body.
5. **Why recreation matters:** it restores the ability to work, supports health, gives education and new impressions, and creates jobs and income in resort regions.
6. **Interdisciplinary field.** It uses geography, medicine (balneology), ecology, economics, management and psychology.

| Concept | Main idea | Needs travel? |
|---|---|---|
| Leisure | Free time | No |
| Recreation | Restoring strength in free time | Not always |
| Tourism | Travel outside usual environment | Yes |
| Recreational tourism | Travel for rest and health | Yes |

## Examples
- A family from Astana spending a weekend at Lake Borovoe (Burabay National Park, Akmola Region) — short-term recreational tourism.
- A two-week stay in a sanatorium in Saryagash (Turkestan Region) — long-term health-oriented recreational tourism.
- An evening walk in an Astana city park — recreation, but not tourism.
- International: a summer beach holiday on the Mediterranean coast of Turkey or Spain.
