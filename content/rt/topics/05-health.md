# Topic 5. Health, spa and wellness tourism (balneology, sanatoriums, resorts)

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Health tourism is travel whose main aim is to treat illness, prevent it or improve well-being with the help of natural healing factors and special services. It includes medical (sanatorium-resort) tourism, spa tourism and wellness tourism.

## Key definitions
- **Health (curative) tourism** — travel for treatment and recovery using natural healing resources (mineral waters, muds, climate).
- **Resort** — a developed territory with natural healing resources and the infrastructure needed to use them for treatment and rest.
- **Sanatorium** — a medical-preventive institution at a resort that combines treatment by natural factors, diet, regime and physiotherapy; a typical stay lasts about 2–3 weeks.
- **Balneology** — the science of mineral waters and their use for treatment and prevention.
- **Balneotherapy** — treatment with mineral waters (drinking, baths, showers, inhalations).
- **Spa** — a facility offering water-based procedures, massage and relaxation; the name comes from the town of Spa in Belgium.
- **Wellness** — a lifestyle approach that combines physical activity, healthy food, relaxation and mental balance; focused on prevention, not treatment.

## Main points
**1. Types of health tourism**

| Type | Aim | Example services |
|---|---|---|
| Medical-sanatorium | Treatment of chronic diseases | Doctor's programme, mineral water, mud, physiotherapy |
| Spa | Relaxation, beauty, recovery | Pools, saunas, massage, wraps |
| Wellness | Prevention, healthy lifestyle | Yoga, fitness, detox, diet, meditation |
| Medical tourism (in narrow sense) | Surgery, diagnostics abroad | Clinics, operations |

**2. Natural healing factors**
- **Balneotherapy** (mineral waters), **peloid therapy** (muds), **climatotherapy** (climate, sun, air), **thalassotherapy** (sea water and sea climate), **landscape therapy** (walking routes, terrain cure).

**3. Types of resorts by main healing factor**
- **Balneological** (mineral waters), **mud** resorts, **climatic** (mountain, seaside, forest-steppe), **mixed** (several factors).

**4. Organisation of a sanatorium stay**
1. Arrival and medical examination.
2. Individual treatment plan (procedures, diet, exercise).
3. Course of treatment, usually 12–21 days.
4. Final examination and recommendations.

**5. Trends**
- From purely medical stays to combined "treatment + rest" programmes.
- Growth of short wellness and spa breaks, hotel spas and day spas.
- Ageing population and stress in cities increase demand.

## Examples
- **Kazakhstan:** Saryagash (Turkestan Region) — the best known balneological resort area of the country; Burabay (Shchuchinsk–Borovoe resort area) — climatic resort with pine forests and lakes, many sanatoriums and wellness hotels; Moiyldy (Pavlodar) — mud treatment; Rakhmanov springs (East Kazakhstan) — mountain sanatorium.
- **World:** Karlovy Vary (Czech Republic), Baden-Baden (Germany) — both among "The Great Spas of Europe" UNESCO World Heritage site (2021); Dead Sea resorts (Israel, Jordan); Caucasian Mineral Waters (Kislovodsk, Pyatigorsk, Essentuki); thermal resorts of Hungary.

- List natural healing factors and the matching therapies.
- Classify resorts by healing factor.
- Explain how a sanatorium stay is organised.
- Give Kazakhstan (Saryagash, Burabay) and world (Karlovy Vary, Dead Sea) examples.
