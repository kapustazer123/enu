# Topic 4. Natural recreational resources: climate, water bodies, landscapes, mineral waters and muds

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Natural recreational resources are parts of nature that are used for rest and treatment: climate, water bodies, relief and landscapes, vegetation, mineral waters and therapeutic muds. They are the base of most recreational regions and resorts.

## Key definitions
- **Climatic resources** — weather and climate conditions (temperature, sunshine, humidity, wind, snow cover) that make a place comfortable or healing.
- **Bioclimatic comfort** — the state in which the human body does not need to strain to keep its heat balance.
- **Hydromineral resources** — mineral (medicinal) underground waters used for drinking cures and baths.
- **Mineral water** — natural underground water with a raised content of salts, gases or special elements that has an effect on the body.
- **Therapeutic muds (peloids)** — natural mud-like deposits (silt, peat, sapropel) with healing properties, used for mud therapy.
- **Landscape resources** — combinations of relief, water and vegetation with aesthetic and recreational value.

## Main points
**1. Climate**
- Decides the length of the season (summer beach season, winter ski season).
- Climate therapy: heliotherapy (sun), aerotherapy (air), thalassotherapy (sea climate and sea water), mountain climate therapy.
- Assessed by comfort indices (temperature, humidity, wind), number of sunny days, period of stable snow cover.

**2. Water bodies**
- Seas, lakes, reservoirs and rivers are used for swimming, beach rest, boating, fishing and rafting.
- Key indicators: water temperature, length of the bathing season, water quality, beach type (sand, pebble), shore accessibility.

**3. Relief and landscapes**
- Mountains: skiing, mountaineering, trekking; plains and steppe: horse riding, eco-tours; canyons and rock formations: sightseeing.
- Forests have health value (clean air, phytoncides) and aesthetic value.

**4. Mineral waters — classification**

| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| Mineralisation (drinking use, Soviet/CIS practice) | Table (below about 1 g/l), medical-table (about 1–10 g/l), medicinal (above about 10 g/l) |
| Gas composition | Carbonic (CO2), hydrogen sulphide, radon, nitrogen |
| Chemical composition | Hydrocarbonate, chloride, sulphate and mixed |
| Temperature | Cold, warm (sub-thermal), thermal, hot |
| Special components | Iodine-bromine, iron, silicon |

**5. Therapeutic muds**
- Types: silt-sulphide muds (salt lakes), peat muds (bogs), sapropel (fresh-water lakes), hill (volcanic) muds.
- Used in mud therapy (peloid therapy) for joint, skin and gynaecological diseases.
- Muds and mineral waters are **exhaustible** — their use must be controlled.

## Examples
- **Climate:** pine-forest climate of Burabay and Bayanaul (Pavlodar Region); dry, sunny climate of south Kazakhstan.
- **Water bodies:** Lake Alakol (saline water, believed to have healing effect), Lake Balkhash (half fresh, half saline), Kapshagay reservoir near Almaty, the Caspian Sea coast near Aktau.
- **Landscapes:** Charyn Canyon, Kolsai lakes, the Altai mountains in East Kazakhstan.
- **Mineral waters:** Saryagash (Turkestan Region); Rakhmanov springs in the Altai (radon waters).
- **Muds:** mud therapy at salt lakes in Pavlodar Region (Moiyldy sanatorium).
- **World:** Dead Sea (salt and mud), Karlovy Vary (thermal waters), Alps (ski climate).
