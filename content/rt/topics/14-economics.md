# Topic 14. Economics and management of recreational enterprises; seasonality

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Recreational enterprises (sanatoriums, resort hotels, recreation bases, ski resorts, camps) must cover their costs and earn profit while protecting the resources they use. Their biggest economic problem is seasonality: demand is concentrated in a few months of the year.

## Key definitions
- **Recreational enterprise** — an organisation that provides recreational services: accommodation, treatment, food, sport and leisure.
- **Seasonality** — regular, repeated changes in demand during the year caused by climate, holidays and school calendars.
- **Peak (high) season / off-season (low season) / shoulder season** — periods of maximum demand, minimum demand and medium demand.
- **Occupancy rate** — the share of available beds or rooms that are sold in a period.
- **Fixed costs** — costs that do not depend on the number of guests (building maintenance, heating, permanent staff, taxes).
- **Variable costs** — costs that change with the number of guests (food, linen, treatment materials).
- **Break-even point** — the number of guests (or revenue) at which income equals total costs.

## Main points
**1. Main economic indicators**
- Revenue, costs, profit and profitability.
- Occupancy rate, average length of stay, average price per bed-day (or room-night).
- Number of bed-days (guest-nights) per year.

**2. Causes of seasonality**
- **Natural:** climate (beach season only in summer, ski season only in winter).
- **Institutional:** school holidays, public holidays, timing of paid leave.
- **Social and cultural:** traditions, fashion.

**3. Effects of seasonality**
- Low occupancy and losses in the off-season; buildings and staff are idle.
- Overcrowding and high prices in peak season, pressure on nature.
- Temporary jobs and loss of qualified staff.

**4. Ways to reduce seasonality**
- All-season products: winter activities at lake resorts, summer hiking at ski resorts.
- Health and wellness services that do not depend on weather.
- Events, festivals and conferences (MICE) in the low season.
- Flexible pricing and discounts in the low season; packages for seniors and corporate clients.
- Indoor infrastructure: pools, spa, aquaparks.

**5. Management of a recreational enterprise**
- **Functions:** planning, organisation, motivation, control.
- **Areas:** service quality, marketing and sales, staff management, finance, safety, environmental management.
- **Pricing methods:** cost-based (costs + mark-up), demand-based, competitor-based; dynamic pricing by season.
- **Sources of finance:** own funds, bank loans, investors, state support and public-private partnership.

**6. Simple break-even formula**
Break-even number of guests = Fixed costs / (Price per guest − Variable cost per guest).

## Examples
- Lake resorts at Alakol, Balkhash and Kapshagay mostly operate in summer and close in winter — strong seasonality.
- Shymbulak uses summer activities (cable car, hiking, events) to reduce dependence on the ski season.
- Burabay works all year thanks to sanatoriums, spa hotels and winter activities.
- Sanatoriums in Saryagash receive guests in all seasons because treatment does not depend on beach weather.

- Explain seasonality: causes and effects.
- Propose at least four measures to reduce seasonality, with examples.
- Describe management functions and pricing methods.
- Show the break-even formula with a simple example from your project.
