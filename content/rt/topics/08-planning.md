# Topic 8. Recreational zoning and territorial planning

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Recreational zoning divides a territory into parts with different recreational functions and rules of use. Territorial planning decides where to place resorts, routes and infrastructure so that recreation is comfortable, profitable and does not destroy the resources it depends on.

## Key definitions
- **Recreational zoning (functional zoning)** — dividing a territory into zones with different purposes and permitted activities (protection, recreation, service, economic use).
- **Recreational regionalisation** — dividing a country into recreational regions that differ in resources, specialisation and level of development.
- **Recreational area / zone** — a territory with resources and infrastructure used mainly for recreation.
- **Territorial planning** — the process of deciding the future use of land: where to build, what to protect, how to link objects by transport and services.
- **Master plan** — the main planning document of a resort or recreation area that shows zones, buildings, roads and networks.
- **Recreation Opportunity Spectrum (ROS)** — a planning tool that classifies land from primitive (wild) to urban settings, so that each area offers a different type of experience.

## Main points
**1. Levels of recreational planning**
- National (state concept and programmes of tourism development, list of priority destinations).
- Regional (regional development plans, resort zones).
- Local (master plan of a resort, national park, recreation base).

**2. Principles of planning**
- Sustainability: use resources without exhausting them.
- Concentration of building in service zones and protection of the most valuable sites.
- Accessibility: transport links from cities.
- Multifunctionality: summer and winter use to reduce seasonality.
- Participation of local communities and stakeholders.

**3. Typical functional zones of a recreational territory**

| Zone | Use |
|---|---|
| Core / protected zone | Strict protection, science only |
| Buffer (ecological stabilisation) zone | Limited visits on fixed trails |
| Recreation zone | Beaches, trails, picnic sites, camping |
| Service zone | Hotels, sanatoriums, restaurants, parking |
| Economic / residential zone | Villages, farming, local business |

**4. Steps of planning**
1. Inventory of resources and assessment (Topic 3).
2. Study of demand and markets.
3. Calculation of carrying capacity (Topic 7).
4. Zoning and selection of sites for objects.
5. Infrastructure plan (roads, water, energy, waste).
6. Economic assessment and implementation plan; monitoring.

**5. Recreational regionalisation of Kazakhstan** (approach used in textbooks): regions are grouped by resources — e.g. the south-east mountain and lake region (Almaty, Zhetisu), the northern forest-lake region (Burabay, Kokshetau), the central steppe and lake region (Karkaraly, Balkhash), the eastern Altai region, the western Caspian region, the southern balneological region (Saryagash).

## Examples
- **Burabay:** the Shchuchinsk–Borovoe resort zone is a priority planning area with a national park, hotels and sanatoriums.
- **Kazakhstan national parks** are divided into functional zones by law — protected core, ecological stabilisation, tourist and recreational activity, and limited economic activity.
- **World:** UNESCO biosphere reserves use the three-zone model (core, buffer, transition); resort master plans in Turkey's Antalya coast concentrated hotels along the shore.

- Name the levels and principles of planning.
- Draw or describe a table of functional zones.
- List the planning steps in order.
- Illustrate with the zones of a Kazakhstan national park or the Burabay resort zone.
