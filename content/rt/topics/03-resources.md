# Topic 3. Recreational resources: classification and assessment methods

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Recreational resources are natural and human-made objects and phenomena that can be used for rest, treatment and tourism. They are classified by origin and use, and they are assessed to decide which activities a territory suits best.

## Key definitions
- **Recreational resources** — elements of the natural and cultural environment that have comfort, uniqueness, aesthetic or healing value and can be used to organise recreation.
- **Recreational potential** — the total capacity of a territory's resources to satisfy recreational needs.
- **Assessment of recreational resources** — determining how suitable a resource is for a given type of recreation.
- **Point (score) method** — each feature is given points on a scale, and points are added to get the total value.

## Main points
**1. Properties of recreational resources**
- Attractiveness (aesthetic value), comfort, uniqueness, accessibility, capacity, reliability (season length), and the possibility of restoration.

**2. Classification**

| Basis | Groups |
|---|---|
| Origin | Natural (climate, relief, water, vegetation, mineral waters, muds); cultural-historical (monuments, museums, traditions); socio-economic (infrastructure, labour) |
| Use | Health and medical; sports; educational; entertainment |
| Renewability | Renewable (forests, beaches), relatively renewable, non-renewable (historical monuments) |
| Exhaustibility | Exhaustible (mineral water deposits, muds) and practically inexhaustible (climate, sun) |
| Scale | Local, regional, national, global significance |

**3. Types of assessment (classic triad of recreational geography)**
- **Medical-biological (physiological)** — how the environment affects the human body: climate comfort, temperature, humidity, wind, sunshine, healing properties of water and mud.
- **Psychological-aesthetic** — how attractive and emotionally valuable the landscape is: variety of relief, presence of water, views, uniqueness.
- **Technological** — how suitable the territory is for a certain activity: slope steepness for skiing, beach width and water temperature for swimming, trail conditions for hiking.

**4. Steps of assessment**
1. Choose the subject (who is the holidaymaker) and the activity.
2. Choose the criteria and indicators.
3. Give points (e.g. 1–3 or 1–5) for each indicator; weight them if needed.
4. Add the points and rank areas as very favourable, favourable, less favourable or unfavourable.
5. Map the results.

**5. Other methods:** expert evaluation, sociological surveys of tourists, cartographic method, comparison with standards, economic valuation (e.g. travel-cost method).

## Examples
- Shymbulak (near Almaty) scores high in a technological assessment for alpine skiing (snow cover, slopes, lifts).
- Kolsai Lakes and Charyn Canyon score high in psychological-aesthetic assessment (unique landscapes).
- Saryagash scores high in medical-biological assessment because of its mineral waters.
- A historical object such as the Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi in Turkistan (UNESCO World Heritage) is a non-renewable cultural resource.

## How to answer in the exam
- Give a definition of recreational resources and their main properties.
- Present the classification in a table (origin, use, renewability, scale).
- Explain the three types of assessment with one indicator each.
- Describe the steps of the point method.
- Use a Kazakhstan example for each assessment type.
