# Topic 2. Recreational needs and the functions of recreation

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Recreational needs are people's needs to rest, restore health and develop themselves in free time; they create demand for recreational tourism. Recreation answers these needs through its medical (health), educational, sports and cultural functions.

## Key definitions
- **Recreational need** — the need of a person or society to restore physical and mental strength and to develop personally in free time.
- **Recreational demand** — the recreational need supported by free time and money, i.e. the real wish to buy a recreational service.
- **Function of recreation** — the role recreation plays for a person and for society (health, education, sport, culture, economy).
- **Motivation** — the inner reason that makes a person choose a certain type of rest (to relax, to be healthy, to learn, to have adventure).

## Main points
**1. What shapes recreational needs**
- **Social and economic factors:** income, length of paid holiday, urbanisation (city people need nature more), level of education.
- **Demographic factors:** age, sex, family status (families with children, young people, pensioners have different needs).
- **Working conditions:** heavy physical work needs passive rest; mental and office work needs active rest and movement.
- **Health status:** people with chronic illness need medical recreation.
- **Fashion and culture:** new trends such as wellness, glamping, adventure travel.

Needs can be **biological** (rest, sleep, movement, fresh air, sun), **social** (communication, prestige) and **spiritual** (knowledge, beauty, self-development). Maslow's hierarchy of needs is often used to explain these levels.

**2. Functions of recreation**

| Function | What it does | Typical activities |
|---|---|---|
| Medical / health (medical-biological) | Treats, prevents disease and restores health | Sanatorium treatment, mineral water and mud therapy, climate therapy |
| Educational (cognitive) | Gives new knowledge and impressions | Sightseeing, eco-trails, museum visits |
| Sports | Develops physical fitness and skills | Hiking, skiing, rafting, cycling |
| Cultural (socio-cultural) | Develops the personality, values, communication | Festivals, ethnographic tours, theatre |
| Economic (for society) | Restores the ability to work; creates jobs and income | All recreational services |

- The **medical function** is divided into **curative** (treatment of illness) and **health-improving** (prevention, strengthening the body).
- The **educational function** is strongest in excursion and ecological tourism.
- The **sports function** links recreation with active and adventure tourism.
- The **cultural function** includes the **upbringing** role: patriotism, respect for nature and heritage.

**3. Recreational needs change over time.** In the 20th century mass beach and sanatorium holidays dominated; today demand is growing for short trips, active rest, wellness and nature experiences.

## Examples
- An office worker from Almaty chooses a hiking weekend in Ile-Alatau National Park — sports and health function.
- Pensioners take a course of treatment in a sanatorium with mineral waters — medical function.
- School groups visit Charyn Canyon and learn about geology — educational function.
- Visiting the Nauryz celebrations or an ethno-village — cultural function.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define recreational need and separate it from recreational demand.
- List the factors that shape needs (social, economic, demographic, health, work).
- Present the functions in a table: medical, educational, sports, cultural (plus economic).
- Give one activity example for each function, if possible from Kazakhstan.
