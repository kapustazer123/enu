# Topic 9. Protected natural areas and ecotourism (national parks, reserves)

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Protected natural areas (PNAs) keep valuable ecosystems, landscapes and species for the future. Some of them, especially national parks, are open for regulated recreation and ecotourism, which supports conservation and the local economy.

## Key definitions
- **Protected natural area (PNA)** — a territory of land or water with special legal protection because of its ecological, scientific, cultural or recreational value.
- **Nature reserve (zapovednik)** — the strictest type of PNA; economic use and mass tourism are forbidden, the main aim is science and conservation.
- **National park** — a PNA that protects nature and at the same time is used for education, science and regulated recreation and tourism.
- **Biosphere reserve** — an area in UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme with a core, buffer and transition zone.
- **Ecotourism** — responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the well-being of local people (The International Ecotourism Society definition, 1990; later versions add interpretation and education).

## Main points
**1. IUCN categories of protected areas**

| Category | Name |
|---|---|
| Ia / Ib | Strict nature reserve / Wilderness area |
| II | National park |
| III | Natural monument or feature |
| IV | Habitat / species management area |
| V | Protected landscape / seascape |
| VI | Protected area with sustainable use of natural resources |

**2. Types of PNAs in Kazakhstan** (by national law): state nature reserves, state national nature parks, state natural parks, nature reserves of limited use (zakazniks), natural monuments, zoological parks and botanical gardens, and others.

**3. Principles of ecotourism**
- Travel to relatively undisturbed nature.
- Low impact: small groups, marked trails, no litter.
- Education and interpretation (guides, visitor centres, information boards).
- Benefits for conservation (fees fund protection).
- Benefits and jobs for local communities (guest houses, guides, crafts).

**4. Forms of ecotourism**
- Hiking on eco-trails, bird watching, wildlife observation, botanical and geological tours, nature photography, scientific tourism, volunteering.

**5. Problems**
- Uncontrolled visitors, litter and fires; weak infrastructure; risk that "eco" is only a marketing label (greenwashing).

## Examples
- **Kazakhstan:**
  - Aksu-Zhabagly Nature Reserve (1926) — the oldest reserve in Central Asia; part of the Western Tien-Shan UNESCO site.
  - Korgalzhyn and Naurzum reserves — the UNESCO World Heritage site "Saryarka — Steppe and Lakes of Northern Kazakhstan" (2008); famous for flamingos and migratory birds.
  - Bayanaul National Park (1985) — the first national park of Kazakhstan.
  - Ile-Alatau (1996), Altyn-Emel, Charyn (2004), Kolsai Lakes (2007), Burabay, Katon-Karagay — popular national parks.
- **World:** Yellowstone (USA, 1872) — the first national park in the world; Costa Rica is a well-known ecotourism destination; Kenya's safari parks.

- Give the IUCN categories.
