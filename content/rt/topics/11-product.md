# Topic 11. Designing recreational tourism products and programmes

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

A recreational tourism product is a set of services and experiences that meets a tourist's need for rest, health or active leisure. Designing it means choosing a target group, a resource, a programme for each day, the services and the price, and checking safety and sustainability. This topic is the core of the course project.

## Key definitions
- **Tourism product** — a combination of services (accommodation, transport, food, excursions, treatment, leisure) and impressions sold to the tourist as one offer.
- **Recreational programme** — the day-by-day plan of activities in a tour or at a resort (treatment, sport, excursions, animation, free time).
- **Tour package** — a pre-arranged combination of at least transport and accommodation sold at one price.
- **Target segment** — a group of customers with similar needs (families, youth, seniors, corporate groups).
- **Animation** — organised leisure and entertainment programmes for guests.
- **Route (itinerary)** — the path of the tour with points, distances and time.

## Main points
**1. Structure of a recreational product**
- **Core:** the main benefit (health, relaxation, adventure, knowledge).
- **Actual product:** accommodation, food, transport, programme, guide, equipment.
- **Augmented product:** transfers, insurance, photo service, souvenirs, loyalty discounts.

**2. Steps of product design**
1. **Idea and market research** — demand, trends, competitors.
2. **Choice of target segment** and their needs (Topic 2).
3. **Choice and assessment of resources** (Topics 3–4).
4. **Concept and name** — the main theme (e.g. "Wellness weekend in Burabay").
5. **Route and programme** — day-by-day schedule, balance of activity and rest.
6. **Selection of suppliers** — hotel, transport, guides, catering.
7. **Costing and pricing** — fixed and variable costs, mark-up, price per person.
8. **Safety and risk plan** — insurance, first aid, weather plan, permits for national parks.
9. **Promotion and sales** — channels, social media, partners.
10. **Testing (pilot / familiarisation tour)** and improvement based on feedback.

**3. Principles of a good recreational programme**
- Matches the needs and physical abilities of the target group.
- Alternates loads: active days followed by easier days.
- Uses local resources, food and culture.
- Respects carrying capacity and the rules of protected areas.
- Is clear, realistic in time and distances, and safe.

**4. Example of a programme table (format)**

| Day | Morning | Afternoon | Evening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Transfer, check-in | Walk on eco-trail | Dinner, briefing |
| 2 | Hiking / treatment | Lake rest | Cultural evening |
| 3 | Excursion | Free time, souvenirs | Departure |

**5. Evaluation of the product:** demand, price competitiveness, profitability, sustainability, customer satisfaction.

## Examples
- A 3-day wellness tour to Burabay for office workers from Astana: forest walks, spa procedures, healthy food, a visit to Okzhetpes rock.
- A 2-day active tour from Almaty: Charyn Canyon, Kolsai lakes and Kaindy lake, with guest-house stay in a village.
- A 12-day sanatorium programme in Saryagash for seniors.
- A family summer package to Alakol with beach rest and children's animation.
