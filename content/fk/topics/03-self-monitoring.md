# Topic 3. Self-Monitoring in Physical Education

> **Based on the course syllabus (in Kazakh) and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture.

Self-monitoring is regular observation of your own health and reaction to exercise. It helps you choose a safe load and notice tiredness or overtraining early.

## Key definitions
- **Self-monitoring (өзін-өзі бақылау)** — systematic observation of your body's state during physical activity, usually written in a self-monitoring diary.
- **Subjective indicators** — what you feel: well-being, mood, sleep, appetite, desire to train, pain.
- **Objective indicators** — what you can measure: heart rate, blood pressure, body weight, lung capacity, test results.
- **Vital capacity (lung capacity)** — the largest volume of air you can breathe out after the deepest breath in; measured with a spirometer.

## Main points
**How to measure heart rate (pulse):** place two fingers on the wrist (radial artery) or the side of the neck; count beats for 10 s and multiply by 6, or count for 15 s and multiply by 4.

| Indicator | Common reference for healthy adults |
|---|---|
| Resting heart rate | about 60–100 beats/min (trained people often lower) |
| Blood pressure | about 120/80 mm Hg is normal; 140/90 or higher is high |
| Maximum heart rate (rough estimate) | 220 − age |
| Recovery | after moderate load, pulse should come back close to resting level within a few minutes |

**Simple functional test — orthostatic test:** measure pulse lying down, then stand up and measure again. A small rise is normal; a very large rise is a sign of fatigue or poor condition.

**External signs of fatigue:**

| Sign | Light fatigue | Strong fatigue |
|---|---|---|
| Skin colour | slight redness | strong redness or paleness |
| Sweating | moderate | heavy |
| Breathing | faster, even | very fast, irregular, through the mouth |
| Movements | confident | unsteady, poor coordination |
| Attention | normal | slow reactions, mistakes |

If you see strong fatigue signs, dizziness, chest pain or nausea, stop exercising and tell the teacher.

## Examples
- A simple diary row: date, sleep hours, mood, resting pulse in the morning, pulse after class, weight, notes.

## How to use this for the credit
- Practise measuring your own pulse quickly and correctly.
- Know the difference between subjective and objective indicators.
- Use the diary while preparing for the fitness standards (Topic 8).
