# Topic 4. Assessing Physical Development

> **Based on the course syllabus (in Kazakh) and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture.

Physical development is the state of the body's size, shape and functional abilities. The syllabus asks students to take simple anthropometric measurements, calculate indices, compare them with standards and make a personal health passport.

## Key definitions
- **Anthropometry** — measuring the human body: height, weight, chest circumference, hand strength.
- **Index** — a simple formula that compares two measurements, for example weight with height.
- **Dynamometer** — a device for measuring hand-grip strength in kilograms.
- **Personal health passport (жеке денсаулық төлқұжаты)** — your own record of measurements, indices and test results over time.

## Main points
**Basic measurements (from the syllabus):**
1. **Height** — standing straight, barefoot, heels, buttocks and back touching the stadiometer.
2. **Weight** — in the morning, on an empty stomach, in light clothes.
3. **Chest circumference** — at rest, at full inhalation and at full exhalation; the difference shows chest mobility.
4. **Hand strength** — with a hand dynamometer, usually the best of several tries for each hand.

**Common indices:**

| Index | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Body Mass Index (BMI) | weight (kg) ÷ height (m)² | WHO: below 18.5 underweight, 18.5–24.9 normal, 25–29.9 overweight, 30+ obesity |
| Weight-height (Quetelet) index | weight (g) ÷ height (cm) | used in many PE textbooks; ask the teacher for the norms used |
| Strength index | hand strength (kg) ÷ body weight (kg) × 100% | shows strength relative to body weight |

**Worked example:** height 1.70 m, weight 65 kg. BMI = 65 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70) = 65 ÷ 2.89 ≈ 22.5 — normal range. Quetelet index = 65 000 g ÷ 170 cm ≈ 382 g/cm. If grip strength is 40 kg, strength index = 40 ÷ 65 × 100 ≈ 62%.

**Comparing with standards:** indices are rough guides. A muscular athlete can have a high BMI without extra fat, so always look at several indicators together.

## Examples
- Repeat your measurements at the start and end of the semester and write both in your health passport to see progress.

## How to use this for the credit
- Be able to calculate BMI and the strength index from your own data.
- Know what each basic measurement shows and how it is taken.

**Facts to check:** the exact norms for the Quetelet and strength indices used by your department.
