# Topic 4. Service culture and standards of guest service

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Service culture is the shared attitude in a company that puts the guest's needs at the centre of every job. Service standards turn this culture into clear, measurable steps that every employee follows.

## Key definitions
- **Service culture** — the values, beliefs and habits in an organisation that support excellent guest service.
- **Service standard** — a written, measurable rule for how a service is delivered (e.g. "answer the phone within three rings").
- **Standard operating procedure (SOP)** — a detailed step-by-step description of a task (check-in, room cleaning, table service).
- **Moment of truth** — any contact in which the guest forms an impression of the company (term popularised by Jan Carlzon of SAS airline).
- **Empowerment** — giving front-line staff the authority to solve guest problems without waiting for a manager.
- **Internal customer** — a colleague or department you serve inside the organisation.

## Main points
1. **Features of hospitality services:** intangibility, inseparability (produced and consumed at the same time), variability (quality depends on people), perishability (an empty room tonight cannot be sold tomorrow). These features make people and standards critical.
2. **Elements of a strong service culture:**
   - clear mission and values;
   - leaders who model service behaviour;
   - careful hiring for attitude, then training for skills;
   - empowerment and recognition of staff;
   - listening to guest feedback.
3. **SERVQUAL model** (Parasuraman, Zeithaml, Berry) — five dimensions of service quality, remembered as **RATER**:

| Dimension | Meaning | Hotel example |
|---|---|---|
| Reliability | Doing what was promised, correctly | The booked room type is ready |
| Assurance | Knowledge, courtesy, trust | Competent, polite receptionist |
| Tangibles | Physical environment, appearance | Clean lobby, neat uniforms |
| Empathy | Individual attention and care | Remembering a guest's preferences |
| Responsiveness | Willingness to help quickly | Fast reaction to a request |

4. **Typical guest service standards:** greeting within seconds, eye contact and smile, using the guest's name, offering help proactively, accurate information, fast check-in/check-out, clean rooms, and a warm farewell.
5. **Ethical side of standards:** standards guarantee **equal treatment** of all guests; they must not become robotic — genuine care is more important than scripts.
6. **Measuring service:** guest satisfaction surveys, online reviews, mystery shoppers, complaint statistics.

## Examples
- Ritz-Carlton is widely known for empowering employees to spend money (a set amount per guest) to solve a problem without asking a manager.
- A restaurant in Almaty may set a standard: bring the menu within two minutes, take the order within five, and check satisfaction after the first bites.
- Kazakh *qonaqjailylyq* — offering tea and food to every guest — is a cultural model of service culture: the guest should feel welcome and cared for.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define service culture and service standard; explain the link between them.
- Mention the four characteristics of services and why they make culture important.
- Present SERVQUAL (RATER) with hotel examples.
- Explain empowerment and moments of truth.
- Add the ethical point: standards ensure fairness and equal quality for every guest.

**Facts to check:** the exact amount Ritz-Carlton staff may spend per guest (often quoted as USD 2,000).
