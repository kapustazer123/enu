# Topic 15. Accessibility and inclusive service (guests with disabilities, special needs, children, elderly)

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Inclusive service means that every guest, whatever their age, health or abilities, can use hospitality services with dignity and independence. Accessibility is both a legal requirement in many countries and a core ethical value of equal treatment.

## Key definitions
- **Accessibility** — the design of buildings, services and information so that people with disabilities can use them.
- **Inclusive service** — service adapted to the diverse needs of all guests without discrimination.
- **Disability** — a long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairment which, in interaction with barriers, may limit full participation in society (social model, as in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, 2006).
- **Universal design** — designing products and spaces usable by all people as far as possible, without special adaptation.
- **Reasonable accommodation** — necessary changes or adjustments that allow a person with a disability to use a service, as long as they are not an unreasonable burden.
- **Accessible tourism** — tourism that allows people with access needs to travel independently, equally and with dignity.

## Main points
1. **Who needs inclusive service:** guests with mobility, visual, hearing or cognitive disabilities; elderly guests; families with small children; pregnant women; guests with allergies or special diets; guests with temporary injuries.
2. **Physical accessibility in hotels and restaurants:**

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Entrance | Ramps, automatic doors, no steps |
| Rooms | Wider doors, roll-in shower, grab bars, lower bed and switches |
| Signs and information | Large print, Braille, contrasting colours, visual fire alarms |
| Restaurant | Accessible tables, menus in large print, staff help |
| Website | Accessible booking, clear photos and description of accessible features |

3. **Etiquette for serving guests with disabilities:**
   - speak directly to the guest, not to the companion or interpreter;
   - ask before helping ("May I help you?") and accept a "no";
   - do not touch or move a wheelchair, cane or guide dog without permission;
   - for blind guests, introduce yourself and describe the room layout;
   - for deaf guests, face them, speak clearly, use writing or messengers;
   - use respectful "people-first" language ("a guest who uses a wheelchair").
4. **Children and families:** child safety (socket covers, cots, high chairs), children's menus, kids' clubs, patient and friendly attitude; never serve alcohol to minors.
5. **Elderly guests:** clear and calm communication, help with luggage, rooms near lifts, good lighting, health and medication needs, no condescending tone.
6. **Ethical basis:** dignity, equality, independence and non-discrimination. Inclusion is also good business: accessible hotels attract a large market of travellers with access needs, their families and older travellers.

## Examples
- A hotel in Astana trains reception staff to describe accessible rooms honestly (door width, shower type) instead of just writing "accessible" on the website.
- A restaurant in Almaty keeps a menu in large print and lists the main allergens for each dish.
- A tour operator in Kazakhstan checks that excursion transport and sites have access for wheelchair users before selling a tour to such a client.

- Name the groups of guests with special needs.
- Describe physical accessibility measures and service etiquette for guests with disabilities.
- Add rules for children and elderly guests.
- Conclude with the ethical (dignity, equality) and business arguments.
