# Topic 5. Professional etiquette, appearance and image of hospitality staff

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Professional etiquette is the set of accepted rules of polite behaviour at work. In hospitality, the appearance and manners of staff are part of the service the guest pays for and form the image of the whole company.

## Key definitions
- **Etiquette** — the established rules of polite behaviour in society or in a profession.
- **Business etiquette** — rules of behaviour in professional situations: greetings, introductions, meetings, communication.
- **Professional image** — the impression a person or company creates through appearance, behaviour, speech and competence.
- **Grooming** — personal care and neatness: hair, nails, hygiene, make-up, uniform.
- **Dress code** — the rules about clothing and appearance set by an employer.
- **Corporate identity** — the visual and behavioural style of a company (logo, uniform, interior, manner of service).

## Main points
1. **Why etiquette is an ethical matter:** it expresses respect for the guest, makes service predictable and fair, and protects the dignity of both guest and employee.
2. **Grooming and appearance standards** (typical for hotels and restaurants):

| Area | Typical standard |
|---|---|
| Uniform | Clean, ironed, complete, with name badge |
| Hair | Neat, tied back in food service |
| Hands and nails | Short, clean nails; minimal jewellery (hygiene) |
| Make-up and perfume | Moderate, no strong fragrance |
| Hygiene | Daily shower, fresh breath, no smell of tobacco |
| Posture | Straight, open, no leaning on counters, hands out of pockets |

3. **Rules of behaviour on duty:**
   - greet first, smile, make eye contact;
   - use polite forms of address and the guest's name when known;
   - do not eat, chew gum, use a personal phone or talk privately in front of guests;
   - let the guest pass first, open doors, offer help with luggage;
   - stay calm and neutral; never argue or gossip;
   - respect personal space and do not touch guests unnecessarily.
4. **Etiquette of introductions and business meetings:** a junior is introduced to a senior, a man to a woman (in traditional etiquette), a host introduces guests; handshakes depend on culture and gender norms.
5. **Ethical limits of dress codes:** rules must be connected to hygiene, safety or the brand, must apply equally, and should respect religion, gender and disability where possible (e.g. allowing a headscarf in a corporate style).
6. **Image is built by everyone:** one rude or untidy employee can damage the image created by expensive interiors and advertising.

## Examples
- A doorman at a five-star hotel in Astana greets every guest, opens the door and helps with luggage, creating the first impression of the hotel.
- In Kazakh tradition, younger people greet older people first and offer them the place of honour (*tör*); hospitality staff can use similar respect for elderly guests.
- A waiter with a strong perfume and long decorated nails may break food hygiene standards and spoil the taste experience for guests.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define etiquette, professional image and grooming.
- Explain why etiquette is part of ethics (respect, fairness, dignity).
- List concrete grooming and behaviour standards (use a table).
- Mention the ethical limits of dress codes (non-discrimination, religion).
- Give an example of how staff image affects the company's reputation.
