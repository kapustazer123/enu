# Topic 12. Ethical marketing, advertising and online reviews

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Ethical marketing means promoting hospitality services honestly, so that guests can make informed choices. Because guests cannot test a hotel or tour before buying, truthful advertising and genuine reviews are essential for trust.

## Key definitions
- **Ethical marketing** — marketing that is truthful, fair, respects consumers and society, and avoids manipulation.
- **Misleading (deceptive) advertising** — advertising that creates a false impression through false statements, hidden information or manipulated images.
- **Greenwashing** — presenting a business as more environmentally friendly than it really is.
- **Drip pricing** — showing a low price first and adding compulsory fees later in the booking process.
- **Fake review** — a review not based on a real experience (written by the owner, paid people or competitors).
- **User-generated content (UGC)** — content created by guests: reviews, photos, posts.
- **Influencer marketing** — promotion through popular social media users; ethically requires disclosure of paid cooperation.

## Main points
1. **Principles of ethical marketing:** truthfulness, transparency, respect for privacy, fairness to competitors, social responsibility, and special care for vulnerable groups (children).
2. **Common unethical practices in hospitality marketing:**

| Practice | Example |
|---|---|
| Edited photos | Wide-angle lenses and editing make small rooms look huge; a "sea view" that is partial |
| Hidden fees | Resort fee, city tax or service charge not shown in the advertised price |
| False scarcity | "Only 1 room left!" when it is not true |
| Bait and switch | Advertising a cheap offer that is not really available |
| Greenwashing | "Eco-hotel" label with no real environmental measures |
| Undisclosed influencer posts | A blogger's "honest opinion" that was actually paid |

3. **Online reviews — ethical rules for businesses:**
   - never write fake positive reviews or fake negative reviews about competitors;
   - do not pay or reward guests only for *positive* reviews;
   - do not hide or delete honest negative reviews;
   - reply to reviews politely, without revealing guests' personal data or arguing;
   - use criticism to improve service.
4. **Ethical rules for guests and bloggers:** honest reviews, no blackmail ("give me a discount or I will write a bad review"), disclosure of free stays or payments.
5. **Data in marketing:** collect e-mails and phone numbers only with consent; allow guests to unsubscribe; no spam.
6. **Cultural and social sensitivity:** advertising must not use sexist images, stereotypes or offensive religious or national symbols.

## Examples
- A hotel in Almaty advertises "mountain view" rooms; ethically, it should specify which room categories have the view.
- Platforms such as Booking.com, TripAdvisor and 2GIS publish guest reviews; hotels and restaurants in Kazakhstan respond to them publicly, which shows their service culture.
- A restaurant invites a food blogger for a free dinner; the blogger marks the post as an advertisement or collaboration.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define ethical marketing, misleading advertising, greenwashing and drip pricing.
- Give a table of unethical practices with hospitality examples.
- Explain ethical rules for managing online reviews (both business and guest side).
- Mention influencer disclosure and consent for marketing data.
- Conclude: honest marketing builds long-term trust and loyalty.
