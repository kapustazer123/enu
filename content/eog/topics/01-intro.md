# Topic 1. Introduction: ethics, morality and professional ethics in hospitality

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Service ethics is the set of moral principles that guide how hospitality workers and businesses treat guests, colleagues, partners and society. In hospitality the "product" is largely human contact, so ethics directly shapes service quality and reputation.

## Key definitions
- **Ethics** — the philosophical study of right and wrong, good and bad behaviour; also a system of principles a person or group follows.
- **Morality (morals)** — the values and norms of behaviour accepted in a society or culture (what people actually believe is right).
- **Professional ethics** — the norms and duties of a specific profession (e.g. hotel staff, tour guides, waiters) that go beyond general morality.
- **Business ethics** — ethical principles applied to company decisions: pricing, marketing, employment, environment.
- **Hospitality** — the friendly and generous reception of guests; as an industry: accommodation, food and beverage, events and related tourism services.
- **Ethical dilemma** — a situation where two or more values conflict and every option has a cost.

## Main points
1. **Ethics vs law.** Law sets the minimum that is compulsory and punished by the state. Ethics is wider: many actions are legal but unethical (e.g. hiding extra fees in small print).
2. **Levels of ethics in hospitality:**

| Level | Who | Example issues |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | Individual employee | Honesty, politeness, not taking bribes |
| Professional | Occupation (receptionist, chef, guide) | Confidentiality, food safety, accurate information |
| Organisational | Hotel, restaurant, tour operator | Fair pricing, codes of conduct, treatment of staff |
| Societal / global | Industry and destinations | Sustainability, local communities, human rights |

3. **Why ethics matters especially in hospitality:**
   - Service is **intangible** and produced in direct contact with the guest, so staff behaviour *is* the product.
   - Guests are **vulnerable**: they are away from home, give personal data, money, passports and keys.
   - Staff have **access** to rooms, belongings and private information.
   - Reputation spreads fast through online reviews.
   - Work is often hard (shifts, low pay, tips), which creates temptations.
4. **Functions of service ethics:** regulating behaviour, building guest trust, protecting the company's image, creating a fair workplace, and supporting sustainable tourism.
5. **Core values** usually named in hospitality ethics: honesty, respect, fairness, responsibility, confidentiality, care for guest safety, and tolerance.
6. **Sources of ethical norms:** national culture and traditions, religion, law, international documents (e.g. the UNWTO Global Code of Ethics for Tourism), company codes of conduct and professional associations.

## Examples
- A receptionist sees a famous guest's room number and is asked about it by a journalist. Telling would break confidentiality even if no law is mentioned.
- Kazakh culture has a long tradition of hospitality (*qonaqjailylyq*): a guest is treated with honour and offered the best at the *dastarkhan*. Modern hotels in Astana and Almaty can build on this cultural value, but professional ethics also requires equal treatment of every guest, not only honoured ones.
- An international chain such as Ritz-Carlton expresses its service philosophy in the motto "Ladies and Gentlemen serving Ladies and Gentlemen", stressing mutual respect between staff and guests.
