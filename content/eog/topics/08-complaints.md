# Topic 8. Handling complaints and conflict situations

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

A complaint is a guest's expression of dissatisfaction, and also a chance to fix a problem and win the guest's loyalty. Ethical complaint handling means listening, taking responsibility and solving the problem fairly, not blaming the guest.

## Key definitions
- **Complaint** — an expression of dissatisfaction with a product or service, made orally, in writing or online.
- **Service failure** — a situation when the service does not meet the guest's expectations or the promised standard.
- **Service recovery** — the actions a company takes to correct a service failure and restore guest satisfaction.
- **Service recovery paradox** — the idea that a guest whose problem was solved very well may become more loyal than a guest who had no problem at all.
- **Conflict** — a clash of interests, opinions or emotions between guest and staff (or between staff).
- **Empathy** — understanding and sharing the feelings of another person.

## Main points
1. **Why complaints are valuable:** most unhappy guests do not complain — they just leave and tell others or write a review. A complaint gives free information and a chance to keep the guest.
2. **Types of complaining guests:** the quiet guest, the aggressive guest, the "expert" guest, the chronic complainer, and the guest who exaggerates to get a discount. All must be treated politely; evidence and policy decide compensation.
3. **Popular complaint-handling models:**

| Model | Steps |
|---|---|
| **LEARN** | Listen – Empathize – Apologize – React – Notify (inform the guest and colleagues/managers so the problem is not repeated) |
| **LAST** | Listen – Apologize – Solve – Thank |
| **HEARD** | Hear – Empathize – Apologize – Resolve – Diagnose |

4. **Rules of ethical complaint handling:**
   - stay calm; do not take it personally;
   - move the guest to a quiet place if emotions are strong;
   - never argue, blame colleagues or the guest;
   - apologise for the inconvenience even before the cause is clear;
   - offer a concrete solution and a time frame; follow up;
   - be fair — compensation should match the problem;
   - record the complaint to prevent repetition.
5. **Conflict management styles** (Thomas–Kilmann): competing, avoiding, accommodating, compromising, collaborating. In service, collaborating (finding a win–win solution) is usually best.
6. **Ethical limits:** staff should not accept abuse, threats or harassment; security or a manager should be called. Fake complaints for free services may be refused politely.

## Examples
- A guest in an Astana hotel complains about noise at night. The receptionist listens, apologises, offers a quieter room immediately, informs engineering about the problem, and next morning calls to check the guest slept well (LEARN).
- A restaurant guest finds a hair in the soup. The waiter apologises, removes the dish, brings a new one quickly and does not charge for it, then thanks the guest for telling them (LAST).
- Answering a negative online review: thank the author, apologise, explain what has been changed, invite them to contact the manager directly.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define complaint, service failure and service recovery.
- Explain why complaints are an opportunity (recovery paradox).
- Present at least one model (LEARN or LAST) step by step.
- List rules of ethical complaint handling and the limits (abuse, fraud).
- Illustrate with a hotel or restaurant example.
