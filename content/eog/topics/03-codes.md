# Topic 3. Codes of ethics: UNWTO Global Code of Ethics for Tourism, corporate codes of conduct

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

A code of ethics turns general moral values into written principles for a whole industry or company. In tourism the main international document is the UNWTO Global Code of Ethics for Tourism; companies add their own codes of conduct.

## Key definitions
- **Code of ethics** — a document stating the values and principles an organisation or profession follows (the "why").
- **Code of conduct** — a more practical document with concrete rules of behaviour for employees (the "what to do / not to do").
- **Global Code of Ethics for Tourism (GCET)** — a set of 10 principles for responsible and sustainable tourism, adopted by the UNWTO General Assembly in 1999 (Santiago, Chile) and acknowledged by the UN General Assembly in 2001.
- **World Committee on Tourism Ethics** — the body that promotes and monitors the implementation of the Code.
- **Whistleblowing** — reporting unethical or illegal behaviour inside an organisation.

## Main points
**The 10 articles of the Global Code of Ethics for Tourism:**
1. Tourism's contribution to mutual understanding and respect between peoples and societies.
2. Tourism as a vehicle for individual and collective fulfilment.
3. Tourism as a factor of sustainable development.
4. Tourism as a user of the cultural heritage of mankind and contributor to its enhancement.
5. Tourism as a beneficial activity for host countries and communities.
6. Obligations of stakeholders in tourism development (e.g. honest information to tourists).
7. Right to tourism.
8. Liberty of tourist movements.
9. Rights of workers and entrepreneurs in the tourism industry.
10. Implementation of the principles.

The Code is **voluntary** (not a binding law), but it guides governments, businesses, tourists and communities. It also condemns the sexual exploitation of children in tourism.

**Typical content of a corporate code of conduct:**

| Area | Examples of rules |
|---|---|
| Guests | Respect, non-discrimination, confidentiality, honest information |
| Colleagues | No harassment, teamwork, fair treatment |
| Company assets | No theft, proper use of equipment and data |
| Conflicts of interest | Gifts, bribes, relatives in hiring |
| Society and environment | Legal compliance, sustainability, human rights |
| Reporting | How to report violations, protection of whistleblowers |

**What makes a code effective:** clear and short language; training for all staff; leaders following it; a confidential reporting channel; fair sanctions; regular review.

**Limitations:** a code can become "paper only" if management ignores it; it cannot cover every situation, so staff still need judgment.

## Examples
- International hotel groups (e.g. Marriott, Hilton, Accor) publish codes of conduct covering human rights, anti-bribery and guest privacy.
- A hotel in Astana may include in its staff handbook rules such as "do not give the room number of a guest to anyone" and "gifts above a small value must be reported to the manager".
- Professional associations of guides and tour operators can adopt their own codes based on the GCET principles.
