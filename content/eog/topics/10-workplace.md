# Topic 10. Workplace ethics: teamwork, discrimination, harassment, tips and gifts, confidentiality

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Workplace ethics covers how hospitality employees treat each other and their employer. Good service for guests is only possible when staff work in a fair, respectful and safe environment.

## Key definitions
- **Teamwork** — cooperation of employees toward a common goal, with shared responsibility.
- **Discrimination** — unfair treatment of a person because of gender, age, ethnicity, religion, disability, appearance or other characteristics.
- **Harassment** — unwanted behaviour that violates a person's dignity or creates a hostile environment; **sexual harassment** is unwanted behaviour of a sexual nature.
- **Conflict of interest** — a situation when personal interests could influence professional decisions.
- **Confidentiality** — the duty not to disclose information about guests, colleagues or the company.
- **Gratuity (tip)** — voluntary money given by a guest for service; **service charge** — a fixed percentage added to the bill by the business.

## Main points
1. **Teamwork ethics:** helping colleagues, sharing information between departments (front office – housekeeping – F&B), not blaming others in front of guests, keeping promises to colleagues, respecting shift changes. The idea of the **internal customer**: each department serves the others.
2. **Non-discrimination:** equal access to jobs, training, promotion and pay; hiring based on competence; no jokes or comments about ethnicity, religion, gender or age. Kazakhstan is a multi-ethnic country, so tolerance is especially important.
3. **Harassment:** hospitality staff can face harassment from guests and colleagues (late hours, alcohol, work in guest rooms). Employers should have a clear policy, training, safe reporting channels, panic buttons for room attendants, and support for victims.
4. **Tips and gifts:**

| Situation | Ethical approach |
|---|---|
| Tips | Follow the company policy: tip pooling or individual tips must be transparent and fair |
| Service charge | Must be clearly shown to guests; ethically, it should reach the staff who served |
| Small gifts from guests | Usually allowed if the company policy permits and they are declared |
| Expensive gifts, money from suppliers | Should be refused — they create a conflict of interest or bribery |

5. **Confidentiality:** do not discuss guests (especially famous ones) with friends or on social media; do not share company prices, plans or colleagues' salaries; protect passwords.
6. **Other workplace issues:** honesty with working time and company property, no alcohol on duty, fair scheduling, respect for labour rights (rest, overtime pay), safety at work (kitchen burns, lifting).

## Examples
- A room attendant finds a guest's expensive watch; she hands it to lost and found and records it — not keeping it is basic honesty.
- A supplier offers a purchasing manager in an Astana hotel a "personal bonus" for choosing his products; this is a conflict of interest and must be refused and reported.
- A waiter posts a photo of a celebrity guest's table on Instagram — this breaks confidentiality and company rules.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define discrimination, harassment, conflict of interest and confidentiality.
- Explain teamwork ethics and the internal customer.
- Discuss tips, service charges and gifts with clear rules.
- Explain how employers prevent harassment and discrimination.
- Give a short case and say what the ethical action is.
