# Topic 7. Intercultural ethics: serving international guests; Kazakh hospitality traditions

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Hospitality staff serve guests from many cultures, so they need cultural awareness, respect and tolerance. Kazakhstan has its own strong hospitality tradition, *qonaqjailylyq*, which is a valuable basis for modern service ethics.

## Key definitions
- **Culture** — shared values, beliefs, norms and behaviour of a group.
- **Intercultural competence** — the ability to communicate and behave effectively and respectfully with people from other cultures.
- **Ethnocentrism** — judging other cultures by the standards of one's own and seeing one's own as superior.
- **Stereotype** — a simplified, generalised belief about a group; it can lead to prejudice and discrimination.
- **Cultural relativism** — understanding behaviour in the context of its own culture.
- **High-context vs low-context culture** (E. T. Hall) — in high-context cultures meaning is often indirect and depends on relationships; in low-context cultures communication is direct and explicit.

## Main points
1. **Ethical principles of intercultural service:** respect, non-discrimination, curiosity instead of judgment, adapting service without losing professional standards.
2. **Areas of cultural difference:**

| Area | Examples of differences |
|---|---|
| Greetings | Handshake, bow, no physical contact between genders |
| Food | Halal, kosher, vegetarian, no pork or alcohol |
| Time | Punctuality vs flexible attitude to time |
| Communication | Direct vs indirect, loud vs quiet |
| Religion | Prayer times, fasting (e.g. Ramadan), holidays |
| Personal space | Close vs distant |

3. **Hofstede's cultural dimensions** (power distance, individualism vs collectivism, masculinity vs femininity, uncertainty avoidance, long-term orientation, indulgence) help explain why guests expect different service styles.
4. **How to serve international guests well:** learn basic phrases and customs of main markets, provide information in several languages, offer dietary options, avoid stereotypes, ask politely instead of assuming.

**Kazakh hospitality traditions (*qonaqjailylyq*):**
- A guest is honoured; a proverb says the guest brings blessing to the home.
- **Dastarkhan** — the festive table (and tablecloth) generously laid with food: *baursak*, dried fruit, sweets, tea and main dishes.
- **Tör** — the place of honour, farthest from the entrance, given to the most respected guest or elder.
- **Beshbarmak** — the main meat dish; the boiled sheep's head may be offered to the most honoured guest, who shares portions with others.
- **Tea ceremony** — tea is served in a *kese* (small bowl), often poured not full, so the host can keep refilling it as a sign of care.
- **Shashu** — scattering sweets at celebrations as a wish of happiness.
- **Sarqyt** — food from the dastarkhan given to guests to take home.
- **Qudaiy qonaq** ("God's guest") — the tradition of receiving even an unexpected traveller.

## Examples
- A hotel in Astana prepares halal breakfast options and a prayer direction sign for Muslim guests, and vegetarian options for others.
- A restaurant offers an "ethno" dinner with beshbarmak and tea, explaining customs to foreign guests without forcing them to take part.
- A receptionist avoids assuming that a guest from Asia does not speak English — she asks which language is preferred.

- Name areas of cultural difference and refer to Hofstede or Hall.
- Give practical rules for serving international guests.
