# Topic 13. Ethical dilemmas and decision-making models (case method)

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

An ethical dilemma is a situation where values or duties conflict and no option is perfect. Decision-making models and the case method help hospitality staff analyse such situations step by step instead of acting on emotion.

## Key definitions
- **Ethical dilemma** — a choice between two or more options, each involving some moral cost (e.g. loyalty to a colleague vs honesty to the employer).
- **Ethical issue** — any situation that requires a moral judgment.
- **Stakeholder** — any person or group affected by a decision: guests, employees, owners, suppliers, community, environment.
- **Case method** — a teaching and analysis method in which students study a real or realistic situation, identify the problem and propose a justified solution.
- **Moral courage** — the willingness to act ethically despite pressure or personal risk.

## Main points
1. **Typical dilemmas in hospitality:**
   - a guest offers a large tip for breaking a rule (e.g. giving another guest's room number);
   - a colleague steals food or falsifies working hours;
   - a VIP guest behaves rudely to staff — protect revenue or protect employees?
   - a manager asks to sell rooms with a known problem (noise, repair work) without telling guests;
   - a guest appears very drunk and wants more alcohol;
   - a lost item of high value is found.
2. **Seven-step decision-making model** (general model used in business ethics):
   1. Identify the facts.
   2. Define the ethical issue (which values conflict?).
   3. Identify stakeholders and how each is affected.
   4. List possible options.
   5. Evaluate options using ethical theories, law and the company code.
   6. Choose the best option and act.
   7. Review the result and learn from it.
3. **Quick ethics tests:**

| Test | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Legality test | Is it legal and in line with company policy? |
| Newspaper / social media test | Would I be comfortable if this appeared in the news or online? |
| Mirror test | Can I look at myself in the mirror after this? |
| Golden Rule test | Would I accept this if I were the guest/colleague? |
| Family test | Would I be proud to tell my family? |
| Balance test | Is it fair to all stakeholders in the short and long term? |

The "Is it legal? Is it balanced? How will it make me feel?" questions are known as the ethics check of K. Blanchard and N. V. Peale.
4. **Case method structure:** (1) summary of the situation; (2) main problem; (3) stakeholders; (4) alternatives with pros and cons; (5) recommended solution with ethical justification; (6) prevention measures.
5. **Common mistakes:** looking only at short-term profit, ignoring some stakeholders, following orders blindly, and "everybody does it" thinking.

## Examples
- **Case:** a guest at an Astana hotel leaves without paying for the minibar; a receptionist's friend works in accounting and suggests "just writing it off". Analysis: honesty and duty to the employer conflict with friendship; the ethical option is to report and follow the procedure.
- **Case:** a waiter in Almaty notices that a guest ordered a dish containing nuts and earlier mentioned an allergy. Even if the guest seems annoyed, the waiter must warn him — guest safety comes first.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define an ethical dilemma and give a hospitality example.
- Present a step-by-step decision-making model.
- Apply quick tests (legality, newspaper, Golden Rule).
- When solving a case, follow the structure: facts – issue – stakeholders – options – decision – justification.
- Justify the decision with at least one ethical theory (Topic 2).
