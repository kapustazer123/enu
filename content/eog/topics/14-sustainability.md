# Topic 14. Corporate social responsibility and sustainable hospitality

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Corporate social responsibility (CSR) means that a business takes responsibility for its impact on people, society and the environment, not only for profit. Sustainable hospitality applies this idea to hotels, restaurants and tourism services.

## Key definitions
- **Corporate social responsibility (CSR)** — voluntary commitment of a company to act ethically and contribute to social and environmental well-being.
- **Carroll's CSR pyramid** — four levels of responsibility: economic (be profitable), legal (obey the law), ethical (do what is right and fair), philanthropic (be a good corporate citizen).
- **Sustainable development** — development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs (Brundtland Report, 1987).
- **Triple bottom line** — measuring success by People, Planet and Profit (John Elkington).
- **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** — 17 global goals adopted by the United Nations in 2015 for the period to 2030.
- **Eco-label / green certification** — independent confirmation that a business meets environmental standards (e.g. Green Key).

## Main points
1. **Why CSR matters in hospitality:** hotels use a lot of water, energy and food; tourism affects local communities and cultures; guests and investors increasingly choose responsible businesses.
2. **Environmental practices:**

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Energy | LED lighting, key-card power, solar panels, efficient heating |
| Water | Low-flow showers, towel and linen reuse programmes |
| Waste | Reducing single-use plastic, sorting, composting |
| Food | Local and seasonal products, reducing food waste, donating surplus food safely |
| Transport | Bicycles, electric vehicle chargers, public transport information |

3. **Social practices:** fair wages and training for staff, hiring local people (including youth and people with disabilities), buying from local farmers and artisans, supporting local culture and charity, preventing child exploitation and human trafficking.
4. **Economic responsibility:** honest taxes, fair contracts with suppliers, long-term investment in the destination.
5. **Ethical risks:** **greenwashing** (claims without real action), CSR used only as PR, and shifting costs onto guests without real benefit (e.g. towel reuse only to save money, with no other actions).
6. **Link to the Global Code of Ethics for Tourism:** Article 3 presents tourism as a factor of sustainable development; Article 5 says tourism should benefit host communities.

## Examples
- Kazakhstan hosted **EXPO 2017** in Astana with the theme "Future Energy", which raised public attention to renewable energy and green building.
- Eco-tourism and guesthouses in national parks such as **Burabay** (Akmola Region) and areas around Almaty can involve local families, sell local food and limit environmental damage.
- A hotel restaurant serving kumys, local dairy and meat from nearby farms reduces transport emissions and supports the local economy.
- International chains publish sustainability or ESG reports describing energy, water and community programmes.
