# Topic 11. Ethical leadership and management in hotels and restaurants

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Ethical leadership means that managers act with integrity, treat staff fairly and build an organisation where doing the right thing is normal. In hospitality, the way managers treat employees is reflected in the way employees treat guests.

## Key definitions
- **Leadership** — the ability to influence and guide people toward common goals.
- **Ethical leadership** — leadership based on honesty, fairness, care for people and clear ethical standards, which the leader both shows personally and promotes in others (Brown and Treviño describe the leader as a "moral person" and a "moral manager").
- **Servant leadership** — an approach where the leader's first aim is to serve and develop employees (Robert Greenleaf).
- **Transformational leadership** — inspiring staff with a vision and values, encouraging growth and commitment.
- **Organisational (ethical) climate** — employees' shared perception of what behaviour is expected and rewarded in the company.
- **Tone at the top** — the ethical example set by senior management.

## Main points
1. **Qualities of an ethical leader:** integrity, honesty, fairness, responsibility, respect, consistency between words and actions, openness to criticism.
2. **Leadership styles in hospitality:**

| Style | Description | Ethical risk / benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Autocratic | Leader decides alone | Fast in crises; risk of disrespect, fear |
| Democratic | Staff participate in decisions | Motivation, fairness; slower |
| Laissez-faire | Little control | Risk of chaos and unethical behaviour |
| Servant | Leader serves staff | Strong care for people; staff serve guests better |
| Transformational | Vision and inspiration | High engagement, values-based culture |

3. **Ethical duties of hospitality managers:**
   - fair recruitment, pay, scheduling and promotion;
   - safe working conditions and respect for labour law;
   - clear rules and a code of conduct, applied equally to everyone (including friends and relatives);
   - training in service and ethics;
   - protecting whistleblowers and investigating complaints;
   - honest reporting to owners, guests and authorities.
4. **Building an ethical climate:** lead by example; reward ethical behaviour, not only revenue; discuss ethical cases in briefings; empower staff; never ask employees to lie to guests.
5. **Common managerial temptations:** pressure to hide problems from owners, cutting costs on safety, unpaid overtime, favouritism (nepotism), manipulating reviews or reports.
6. **"Happy staff — happy guests":** many service-profit chain ideas link employee satisfaction to guest satisfaction and loyalty.

## Examples
- A general manager of a hotel in Astana works one shift a month at the front desk to understand staff problems — an example of servant leadership.
- A restaurant manager refuses to tell waiters to "say the fish is fresh" when it is not; instead the dish is removed from the menu.
- A manager investigates a complaint of harassment against a senior chef in the same way as against a junior employee, showing equal application of rules.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define ethical leadership and name its key qualities.
- Compare leadership styles and their ethical risks.
- List the ethical duties of managers toward staff, guests and owners.
- Explain how leaders build an ethical climate ("tone at the top").
- Link employee treatment with guest satisfaction.
