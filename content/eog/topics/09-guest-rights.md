# Topic 9. Guest rights: privacy, data protection, honest pricing, overbooking, safety

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Guests trust hotels and restaurants with their safety, money and personal information. Respecting guest rights is both a legal duty and a central ethical obligation of hospitality businesses.

## Key definitions
- **Guest (consumer) rights** — the rights of a person who buys services: to safety, information, choice, fair treatment and redress (compensation).
- **Privacy** — the right of a guest to be left alone and to keep personal life and information private.
- **Personal data** — any information that identifies a person: name, passport details, phone, card number, room number, stay dates, photos.
- **Data protection** — rules and measures for collecting, storing and using personal data lawfully and securely.
- **Overbooking** — accepting more reservations than available rooms or seats, expecting some no-shows.
- **"Walking" a guest** — relocating a guest with a confirmed reservation to another hotel because of overbooking.
- **Duty of care** — the obligation to take reasonable steps to keep guests safe.

## Main points
1. **Privacy and confidentiality:**
   - never confirm whether a person is staying in the hotel or give a room number to third parties;
   - say the room number quietly or write it, not aloud at the desk;
   - knock and announce before entering a room; respect "Do not disturb";
   - do not look through guests' belongings.
2. **Data protection:** collect only data that is needed; get consent; store passports and card data securely; limit staff access; do not use guest data for marketing without consent. Kazakhstan has a law on personal data and its protection; in the EU the GDPR (2018) sets strict rules that affect hotels serving European guests.
3. **Honest pricing:**
   - display full prices including taxes and fees;
   - avoid **hidden or "drip" fees** (resort fees, service charges added only at the end);
   - no price discrimination by nationality or appearance;
   - honest minibar and restaurant bills.
4. **Overbooking ethics:**

| Unethical practice | Ethical practice |
|---|---|
| Hiding the problem until arrival | Warning the guest early if possible |
| Leaving the guest without a room | Arranging a comparable or better hotel |
| Making the guest pay extra | Paying for the first night, transport and a call home |
| Choosing "weak" guests to walk | Using fair, transparent criteria |

5. **Safety and security:** fire safety (exits, alarms, evacuation plans), food safety and hygiene, safe-deposit boxes, key-card control, CCTV in public areas only, first aid, protection against crime and human trafficking.
6. **Right to redress:** guests must have a clear way to complain and receive fair compensation.

## Examples
- A caller says, "I'm Mr. N's wife, what room is he in?" The receptionist offers to connect the call to the room or take a message, but does not give the number.
- A hotel in Almaty is overbooked during a big conference; it books guests into a partner hotel nearby, pays for the taxi and the night, and offers a priority return the next day.
- A restaurant lists the service charge clearly on the menu instead of adding it silently to the bill.

- Explain privacy and data protection with practical rules.
- Discuss honest pricing and hidden fees.
- Analyse overbooking: why hotels do it and how to do it ethically.
