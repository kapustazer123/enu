# Topic 6. Ethics of communication with guests: verbal, non-verbal, phone, online

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Ethical communication means speaking and writing to guests honestly, respectfully and clearly in every channel. Guests judge a hotel or restaurant not only by what staff say, but by tone, body language and the speed and quality of online replies.

## Key definitions
- **Verbal communication** — the use of words, spoken or written.
- **Non-verbal communication** — messages sent without words: facial expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, distance, tone of voice.
- **Paralanguage** — how something is said: tone, volume, speed, pauses.
- **Active listening** — fully concentrating on the speaker, showing attention, clarifying and summarising what was said.
- **Proxemics** — the use of personal space in communication (intimate, personal, social, public distance — E. T. Hall).
- **Netiquette** — rules of polite behaviour in online communication.

## Main points
1. **Principles of ethical communication:** honesty (no false promises), respect and politeness, clarity, confidentiality, equal treatment, and responsibility for one's words.
2. **Verbal communication rules:**
   - use polite phrases ("Good evening", "May I help you?", "Thank you for waiting");
   - speak clearly, avoid slang and professional jargon;
   - say what you *can* do, not only what you cannot;
   - never use humiliating, sexist or ethnic jokes;
   - adapt language — offer English, Kazakh or Russian when possible.
3. **Non-verbal communication:** smile, open posture, eye contact, nodding, a calm tone. Crossed arms, looking at a phone or sighing show disrespect even when words are polite. Gestures differ between cultures, so neutral gestures are safest.
4. **Telephone etiquette:**

| Step | Standard |
|---|---|
| Answer | Quickly (often within three rings), with a smile in the voice |
| Greeting | Greeting + name of hotel/department + own name + offer of help |
| During the call | Listen actively, take notes, repeat key details (dates, names, numbers) |
| Hold/transfer | Ask permission before putting on hold; explain the transfer |
| End | Summarise, thank the caller, let the guest hang up first |

5. **Online communication (e-mail, messengers, social media, review sites):** reply promptly; use correct grammar and the guest's name; keep a professional tone; never disclose personal data publicly; answer negative reviews calmly and politely and move the details to a private channel.
6. **Ethical traps:** lying to "save" a sale, promising what cannot be delivered, discussing guests with colleagues in public areas, and ignoring guests while chatting with staff.

## Examples
- A receptionist in Almaty answers: "Good afternoon, Hotel X reception, Aigerim speaking. How may I help you?" — then repeats the booking dates to avoid errors.
- A guest writes in a messenger at night; the hotel replies in the morning with an apology for the delay and a full answer — this is better than an automatic message with no follow-up.
- A waiter says "The kitchen needs 25 minutes for this dish" instead of "It will be quick", giving honest information.

## How to answer in the exam
- Define verbal and non-verbal communication and active listening.
- State the ethical principles of communication (honesty, respect, clarity, confidentiality).
- Describe phone etiquette step by step and online rules (netiquette, review replies).
- Mention cultural differences in gestures and personal space.
- Give an example of honest versus misleading communication.

**Facts to check:** the "7–38–55" rule of A. Mehrabian is often quoted about non-verbal communication, but it applies only to special experimental conditions — use it carefully.
