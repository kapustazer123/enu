# Topic 2. Understanding the Cultural Heritage Tourist

> **Short note** — no lecture slides yet; based on the syllabus summary. Will be expanded when the slides arrive.

Who is the heritage tourist? This lecture looks at visitor motivations, demographic patterns and psychological drivers that make heritage tourists different from other travelers, and at how to meet their expectations.

## Key definitions

- **Heritage tourist** – a visitor whose main reason to travel is to experience the history, culture and traditions of a place.
- **Motivation** – the inner reason why a person travels (for example, to learn, to feel a connection to the past, to find family roots).
- **Demographic pattern** – typical characteristics of a group of visitors, such as age, education, income or place of origin.
- **Persona** – a fictional but research-based profile of a typical visitor, used to plan products and services.

## Main points

- Heritage tourists differ from general leisure tourists: their focus is on meaning, learning and connection with the past, not only on rest and entertainment.
- To understand them, we study three things: **who** they are (demographics), **why** they travel (motivations) and **what drives them inside** (psychological drivers).
- There are different types of heritage tourists, for example *nostalgia seekers* and *educational tourists* (independent work for week 2); they behave differently and expect different things.
- A site must know what its visitors seek in order to meet their expectations.
- Seminar task: create a **heritage tourist persona** with demographics, motivations, behaviors and expectations.

## How to answer in the exam

- Define the heritage tourist and contrast with the general leisure tourist (exam question 2).
- Structure your answer: demographics, motivations, psychological drivers, expectations.
- Compare two types of heritage tourists (exam question 8).
- Finish with how a site or tour can respond to these expectations.
