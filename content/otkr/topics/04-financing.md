# Topic 4. Funding Heritage Tourism: Strategies and Sources

> **Short note** — no lecture slides yet; based on the syllabus summary. Will be expanded when the slides arrive.

This lecture introduces international and national funding opportunities for heritage tourism projects. It covers writing effective funding proposals, working with agencies like UNESCO or local ministries, and creating realistic budgets and financial plans.

## Key definitions

- **Funding source** – an organisation that gives money for a project, for example an international agency, a national ministry, a local authority or a private sponsor.
- **Grant** – money given for a specific project that usually does not have to be paid back, but must be spent as agreed.
- **Funding proposal (grant application)** – a document that explains the project and asks a funder for money.
- **Budget** – a plan of all expected costs and incomes of a project.

## Main points

- Funding can come from **international** and **national** levels; the lecture names agencies like **UNESCO** and local ministries.
- A strong proposal clearly shows: **objectives**, **beneficiaries** (who gains from the project), **activities**, **budget breakdown** and **timeline** (seminar task).
- Budgets and financial plans must be **realistic** – funders check that costs match the activities.
- Students research at least 3 active grant or subsidy programs in Kazakhstan or their region, with eligibility, funding size and deadlines (independent work for week 4).
- Seminar task: a **mini-grant proposal** (3–4 pages) for a small local heritage tourism project.

## How to answer in the exam

- Name the main types of funding sources (international, national, local, private).
- List the standard parts of a funding proposal.
- Explain why a realistic budget and timeline matter.
- If possible, give a real program you studied in the independent work as an example.
