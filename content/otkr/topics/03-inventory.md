# Topic 3. Inventory and Evaluation of Cultural Heritage Resources

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Before heritage is used for tourism, we must know what exists, in what condition and how valuable it is. An inventory identifies and maps assets; an evaluation judges their significance, integrity, authenticity and tourism potential.

## Key definitions

- **Heritage inventory** – a systematic list of heritage assets with standard information on each (location, type, date, condition, legal status).
- **Heritage resource audit** – a check of all assets of an area to decide what can be used for tourism, what needs conservation and what should not be promoted.
- **Significance** – why an asset matters and for whom. The **Burra Charter** (Australia ICOMOS, 1979) lists aesthetic, historic, scientific, social and spiritual value; the result is a **statement of significance**.
- **Integrity** – how complete and intact an asset is.
- **Authenticity** – how genuine an asset is. The **Nara Document (1994)** says it must be judged within each culture through form and design, materials, use and function, traditions and techniques, location and setting, spirit and feeling.
- **Cultural landscape** – an area shaped by people and nature together (a UNESCO World Heritage category since 1992): *designed*, *organically evolved* (relict or continuing) and *associative*.

## Main points

**1. Process**
Identify → document → map → evaluate → set priorities → monitor.

**2. Inventory tools**
- Standard inventory forms and a database (one record per asset).
- Desk research: state lists of monuments, UNESCO/ICOMOS classifications, literature, **archives and historical maps** (to find lost places, historic roads and stories, and to check facts).
- Field survey with checklists, photography, drones, 3D scanning.
- **GIS mapping** of clusters, routes, infrastructure and risk zones.
- Community participation: interviews, oral history, participatory mapping.
- Evaluation matrix (scores) and SWOT.

**3. Classification (UNESCO approach)**

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Tangible immovable: monuments, groups of buildings, sites | mausoleums, old quarters, settlements |
| Tangible movable | artefacts, manuscripts, costumes |
| Intangible (5 domains) | epics, music, rituals, nature knowledge, crafts |
| Natural / mixed | landscapes with cultural meaning |

**4. Evaluation matrix (seminar criteria plus common extras)**
Significance (local, national, international) · authenticity · integrity and condition · accessibility (transport, opening, safety) · tourism potential and story value · vulnerability and carrying capacity · community attitude (sacred or private?).

**5. Methodology for assessing authenticity**
Define cultural context → collect evidence (archives, photos, restoration records) → assess each Nara attribute → check that reconstructions are evidence-based and distinguishable (Venice Charter, 1964) → check interpretation and shows (are they staged?) → consult experts and community → score and recommend.

**6. Role of ICOMOS charters**
They give common criteria: Venice Charter (original fabric, minimal intervention), Burra Charter (significance), Nara Document (cultural relativity of authenticity), Cultural Tourism Charter (1999, visitor management and host communities). ICOMOS also evaluates nominations to the World Heritage List.

**7. Non-official and marginalised heritage**
Heritage of minorities, women, workers, deported groups, Soviet and industrial sites is often missing from lists. Challenges: few documents, political and emotional sensitivity, no legal protection or funding, trust and consent issues, disappearing witnesses. Solutions: oral history, participatory mapping, partnership with communities.

## Examples

- **Yasawi mausoleum:** historic, architectural, spiritual and social value → international significance (World Heritage, 2003).
- **Tamgaly:** petroglyphs, settlements and burials form one archaeological cultural landscape (World Heritage, 2004).
- **Five Kazakh ICH examples:** Nauryz, dombra *kui*, *aitys*, yurt- and felt-making, kokpar (also *asyk* games, eagle hunting).
- **Marginalised heritage:** memory sites of deported peoples and camps such as ALZHIR and Karlag.
