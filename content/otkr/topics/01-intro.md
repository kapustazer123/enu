# Topic 1. Introduction to Fundamentals of Tourist and Local History Work

This introductory lecture defines the key concepts of local history and tourism. It shows how historical identity, collective memory and cultural resources shape tourism practices, and why the field is interdisciplinary.

## Key definitions

- **Local history work** – the study of the history, culture, nature and people of one specific place (a town, village or region), and the practical use of this knowledge, for example in museums, education and tourism.
- **Historical identity** – the sense of "who we are" that a community builds from its shared past.
- **Collective memory** – the way a group of people remembers its past together; it decides which events, people and places are seen as important.
- **Cultural resources** – the sites, objects, traditions and stories of a place that can be studied, protected and presented to visitors.

## Main points

- Local history gives tourism its content: the stories, sites and traditions that make a place different from others.
- Identity and memory influence what a community chooses to show to tourists and how it tells its story.
- The field is **interdisciplinary**: it uses history, geography, cultural studies, economics and management together.
- Regions use local history in their tourism policy documents and branding (independent work for week 1).
- Seminar task: a **concept map** that shows the links between local history work, cultural identity, memory and tourism development, using at least 10 academic sources.

## How to answer in the exam

- Start with a clear definition of local history work, then explain why it matters for tourism (exam question 1).
- Name the three building blocks: historical identity, collective memory, cultural resources.
- Stress the interdisciplinary nature of the field.
- Give one example from your own city or region of how local history is used in tourism.
