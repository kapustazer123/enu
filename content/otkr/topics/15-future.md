# Topic 15. The Future of Heritage Tourism

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

Heritage tourism is being reshaped by climate change, digitalisation (including AI), the demand for inclusivity and the empowerment of local communities. Each trend brings both risks and opportunities, and destinations prepare for them with scenario planning.

## Key definitions

- **Trend** – a long-term direction of change that affects many destinations.
- **Megatrend** – a very large global trend (climate change, digitalisation, demographic change, urbanisation).
- **Climate change** – long-term change in temperature and weather patterns, with more heat waves, floods, droughts and storms.
- **Digitalisation** – growing use of digital technology in planning, managing, promoting and experiencing heritage.
- **Artificial intelligence (AI)** – systems that analyse data, predict, recommend and generate text or images.
- **Inclusivity** – heritage that is accessible and meaningful for all: people with disabilities, elderly, children, different cultures and incomes.
- **Community empowerment** – local people have real control over decisions and receive fair benefits.
- **Overtourism** – too many visitors, damaging sites and residents' quality of life.
- **Scenario planning** – imagining several possible futures and preparing flexible strategies (Topic 5).

## Main points

**1. Four key trends: challenges and opportunities**

| Trend | Challenges | Opportunities |
|---|---|---|
| Climate change | damage to stone, mud-brick and wooden structures; heat danger; higher costs | new seasons, slow and domestic travel, climate education at sites |
| Digitalisation and AI | cost, misinformation, digital divide, data privacy | AR/VR, digital twins, online booking, virtual access |
| Inclusivity | expensive adaptation of old buildings | new audiences, better experience for everyone |
| Community empowerment | conflicts of interest, slow decisions | authentic products, local income, acceptance |

**2. Climate change in the next decade**
Physical damage (rain, floods, freeze–thaw, erosion of rock art and mud-brick); shifts of peak seasons; heat risks for visitors; higher conservation and insurance costs; pressure to reduce flights; threats to intangible heritage linked to nature (pastures, water, herding). Responses: climate risk assessment in management plans, monitoring sensors, shade and new opening hours, digital documentation of vulnerable sites.

**3. AI in destination management**
Forecasting visitor flows and supporting timed entry and pricing; analysing sensor and camera data to detect damage or crowding; multilingual chatbots and personal route recommendations; marketing analysis; digitising and translating archives; accessibility (audio description, translation). Risks: invented facts, bias, privacy, loss of guide jobs – humans and communities must stay in control.

**4. Heritage tourism in 2035 – possible picture**
- *Innovations:* hybrid on-site + digital visits, AI personal guides, real-time monitoring of major sites, low-impact "slow" routes, community-led and inclusive experiences as standard.
- *Disruptions:* climate damage and closed seasons, stricter visitor caps, partial virtual substitution of travel, AI misinformation, sudden crises (pandemics, conflicts) – as COVID-19 showed.

**5. Scenario planning for 2035 (seminar)**
Choose 3 global trends → describe how each affects your destination → combine them into a story of the future → identify robust actions that help in any case → present as a future news article or visual.

## Examples

- Mud-brick archaeological sites such as **Otrar** and **Sauran** are vulnerable to heavy rain and erosion; hotter summers in Turkistan region may move the main season to spring and autumn.
- An AI-based app could forecast peak hours at the **Yasawi mausoleum** and answer questions in Kazakh, Russian, English and Turkish, with content checked by historians.
- **"Turkistan 2035" news article:** "Night tours and AR reconstructions of Sauran attract record spring visitors; local families run guesthouses and a booking quota protects the mausoleum."
