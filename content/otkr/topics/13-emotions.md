# Topic 13. Emotions, Memory, and the Heritage Experience

Feelings and personal memories shape the meaning of cultural spaces in the present. Heritage is not a set of frozen monuments but a living process, and it lives wherever the past sparks emotional resonance.

## Key definitions

- **Heritage as a living process** – an active negotiation of the past in the present.
- **Authorized discourse** – the traditional, object-centered view of heritage, led by experts.
- **Emotional turn** – the shift to an experience-centered view, where emotions shape meaning.
- **Difficult heritage** – heritage connected with painful or unjust parts of the past that creates discomfort but also recognition.
- **Intergenerational memory transmission** – passing memories and values from one generation to the next, for example during family visits to museums and memorials.

## Main points

**1. Heritage as a living process**
- **Agency** – visitors actively construct the meaning of a site.
- **Embodiment** – being physically present creates deep resonance.
- **Presentism** – the past is framed through current values.

**2. Paradigm shift in heritage studies**

| Authorized discourse (object-centered) | Emotional turn (experience-centered) |
|---|---|
| Materiality – priority on ancient stones and artifacts | Performance – meaning emerges during the visit |
| Expert monopoly – one authoritative interpretation | Co-creation – personal experience equals the curator's voice |
| Passivity – visitor as a silent student | Affectivity – emotions govern depth of perception |

**3. Three registers of emotional engagement**
- **Affirmation** – reinforcing personal values and beliefs.
- **Empathy** – deep connection with past human lives.
- **Re-evaluation** – discomfort when facing difficult truths.

**4. How the heritage experience forms (4 stages)**
1. **Expectation** – visitors arrive with personal "memory baggage".
2. **Encounter** – embodied, sensory contact with the space.
3. **Resonance** – the emotional response tests the historical narrative.
4. **Meaning** – the visitor forms a renewed position in the present.

**5. Intergenerational memory.** Museums and memorials are catalysts for family dialogue:
- Family narrative – personal stories join the broader history.
- Belonging – roots and generational continuity.
- Living dialogue – shared reflection strengthens family bonds.

**6. Difficult heritage and the politics of recognition**
- **Discomfort** – disrupts comforting myths and clichés.
- **Recognition** – gives voice back to marginalized communities.
- **Implication** – links historical injustice with today's reality.
- **Curation** – designs safe spaces for challenging dialogue.

## Examples

- **Laurajane Smith's study:** 4,502 in-depth interviews in 45 museums around the world showed that visitors come mainly **to feel**, not only to learn.
- A visitor quote (Emotional Heritage Project): "I don't visit museums for dry textbook facts – I come here to feel a living connection with history."
