# Topic 5. Strategic Planning for Heritage Tourism Development

> **Short note** — no lecture slides yet; based on the syllabus summary. Will be expanded when the slides arrive.

What makes a compelling heritage tourism strategy? This lecture covers planning processes, thematic development, stakeholder coordination (hotels, museums, municipalities) and integrating tourism into regional development agendas.

## Key definitions

- **Strategy** – a long-term plan that sets where a destination wants to go and how it will get there.
- **Vision** – a short picture of the desired future of the destination.
- **Mission** – a statement of the purpose of the organisation or destination and what it does to reach the vision.
- **Stakeholders** – all groups that affect or are affected by tourism: hotels, museums, municipalities, residents, businesses.
- **Thematic development** – building the destination's offer around one or more clear heritage themes.

## Main points

- A strategy follows a **planning process**: analyse the situation, set a vision and mission, choose goals, plan actions and measure success.
- **Themes** give the destination a clear and recognisable identity.
- **Stakeholder coordination** is essential: hotels, museums and municipalities must work together, not separately.
- Heritage tourism should be **integrated into regional development agendas**, not planned in isolation.
- Students analyse a foreign heritage tourism strategy: its goals, stakeholders and success indicators (independent work for week 5).
- Seminar task: write a **vision and mission**, 3 strategic goals and a list of stakeholders for a destination.

## How to answer in the exam

- List the key components of a strategic plan (exam question 21).
- Explain what a mission statement is and why it matters (question 24).
- Show how to involve local stakeholders (question 22) and how hotels and restaurants contribute (question 23).
- Link the strategy to regional development.
