# Topic 5. Strategic Planning for Heritage Tourism Development

> **Based on the course annotation and open sources** — not the teacher's lecture. Check key points against the lectures.

A heritage tourism strategy is a long-term plan that says where a destination wants to be, how it will get there, who does what and how success is measured – while protecting heritage. It needs clear themes, coordinated stakeholders and links to regional development.

## Key definitions

- **Strategy / strategic plan** – a long-term (usually 5–10 years) document with vision, goals, actions, resources and indicators.
- **Vision** – a short picture of the desired future of the destination.
- **Mission** – a short statement of purpose: what the destination or organisation does, for whom, how and with what values.
- **SMART goals** – Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
- **SWOT** – Strengths and Weaknesses (internal), Opportunities and Threats (external).
- **PEST(LE)** – Political, Economic, Social, Technological (Legal, Environmental) factors of the external environment.
- **Stakeholders** – everyone who affects or is affected by tourism.
- **DMO** – Destination Management (Marketing) Organisation that coordinates tourism in a place.
- **Scenario planning** – preparing for several possible futures instead of one forecast.

## Main points

**1. Planning process / key components of a plan**
1. **Situation analysis** – heritage audit (Topic 3), visitor and market data, SWOT, PEST, competitors.
2. **Vision and mission.**
3. **Strategic goals** (about 3) and SMART objectives.
4. **Thematic development** – central themes (e.g. "Silk Road", "nomadic civilisation", "spiritual heritage") that link sites into routes and products.
5. **Action plan** – product, infrastructure, interpretation, marketing, training; with responsibilities and timeline.
6. **Budget and funding** (Topic 4).
7. **Sustainability and visitor management** – carrying capacity, conservation rules, community benefit.
8. **Monitoring and KPIs** – satisfaction, length of stay, spending, local jobs, condition of sites, residents' approval.

**2. Mission statement – why it matters**
It gives all partners a common direction, guides decisions (including saying "no" to harmful projects), is the basis for goals and KPIs, balances preservation and tourism, and supports consistent branding.

**3. Stakeholder coordination**

| Stakeholder | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Municipality / akimat | planning, infrastructure, regulation, funding |
| Museums, heritage bodies | conservation, research, interpretation |
| Hotels | heritage accommodation, packages, longer stays |
| Restaurants | food heritage, tastings, local suppliers |
| Tour operators, guides | products, storytelling, distribution |
| Community, artisans, religious bodies | living traditions, consent, hospitality |
| NGOs, universities | expertise, research, training |

**How to involve them:** stakeholder mapping (interest/influence grid) → early consultation (meetings, surveys) → co-design workshops → shared governance (advisory board or DMO) → benefit sharing → transparent communication → monitoring of approval.

**4. Integration into regional development**
Tourism should be part of regional plans for transport, jobs, small business, culture and environment, not a separate project.

**5. Scenario planning**
Focal question and horizon (e.g. 2035) → drivers of change (climate, digitalisation, demography) → two key uncertainties → 2×2 matrix of four scenarios → test which strategies are robust in all → early-warning indicators. It reduces risk of long-term investments and protects fragile heritage.

**6. Key success factors**
Clear unique selling proposition; strong storytelling; capacity controls; partnerships; community involvement and benefit; sustainability and reinvestment in conservation; quality over volume; accessibility; stable funding and governance; monitoring with KPIs.

## Examples

- **Turkistan:** vision "a spiritual and cultural centre of the Turkic world"; goals: extend average stay, train local guides, manage crowds at the Yasawi mausoleum; hotels sell combined tickets with local guides and serve traditional cuisine.
- **Mission example:** "To share the Silk Road heritage of our region in a way that protects its monuments, respects sacred places and brings fair benefits to local people."
