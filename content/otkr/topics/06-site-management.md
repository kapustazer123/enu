# Topic 6. Sustainable Site Management in Heritage Tourism

Site management must balance monument preservation, carrying capacity limits and inclusive public access. The key message of the lecture: sustainability is a continuous adaptive process, not a static state.

## Key definitions

- **Tourism Carrying Capacity (TCC)** – the limit of visitor use a site can take. It has three levels: physical, ecological and social.
- **Limits of Acceptable Change (LAC)** – an adaptive governance framework that sets how much change at a site is acceptable and what to do when the limit is reached.
- **Conservation Management Plan (CMP)** – a strategic plan that assesses a site's significance and vulnerability and sets rules for restoration and use.
- **Outstanding Universal Value (OUV)** – the special value of a site that is mapped in the significance assessment of a CMP.
- **Authenticity** – keeping the real historical substance and meaning of a site, without false reconstruction.

## Main points

**1. The central tension: preservation vs. public access**

| Conservation priority | Visitation priority |
|---|---|
| Minimise physical wear | Enhance public education |
| Protect fragile site microclimates | Generate revenue for site maintenance |

**2. Three levels of carrying capacity (TCC)**
- **Physical** – the maximum number of people the space can hold within safety boundaries.
- **Ecological** – the threshold up to which damage to the environment and the historic fabric is still reversible.
- **Social** – the limit of crowding before visitors stop enjoying the site and residents lose comfort.

**3. LAC framework – four steps**
1. **Define values** – identify the core heritage assets.
2. **Set indicators** – measure, for example, surface abrasion and humidity.
3. **Fix standards** – establish critical impact limits.
4. **Take action** – adjust entry caps and visitor routing.

**4. Visitor flow management controls**
- **Timed entry reservation** – hourly quotas through advance booking.
- **Dynamic tariff pricing** – higher prices at peak hours to shift demand.
- **Spatial micro-zoning** – strict separation of the fragile core and the buffer zones.

**5. Conservation Management Plan (CMP)** – three parts: significance assessment (mapping OUV), vulnerability analysis (environmental and human risks), policy formulation (restoration and usage rules).

**6. Authenticity in interpretation** – Venice Charter principles: minimal physical intervention and preservation of all historical layers. Modern interpretation avoids false historical reconstruction and conveys the *spirit of place* through contextual storytelling.

**7. Inclusive design without site damage**
- Reversible wooden boardwalks protect soil and archaeological layers.
- Tactile 3D models help visually impaired guests.
- Multisensory audio guides offer audio description and sign-language media.

**8. Smart monitoring technologies**
- IoT climate sensors – real-time tracking of humidity and vibration.
- Heatmap crowd tracking – finding visitor bottlenecks through Wi-Fi telemetry.
- BIM digital twins – predicting structural strain and wear.

**9. Community-centric governance** – co-management (residents on advisory boards), economic benefit (local jobs, support for traditional artisans), cultural protection (reducing gentrification and displacement).

## How to answer in the exam

- Start with the tension between preservation and access, then present TCC with its three levels (exam question 28).
- Describe the four LAC steps in order – this shows you understand management as an adaptive cycle.
- Name concrete tools: timed entry, dynamic pricing, micro-zoning, monitoring technologies.
- Mention authenticity (Venice Charter), inclusive design and the role of the community.
- End with the lecture's conclusion: sustainability is a continuous adaptive process.
